# Support Practices and Reported Consequences in Product Development Teams: A Systematic Review Protocol

**Protocol version:** 1.8.
**Protocol date:** 25 October 2026 (Japan Standard Time).

This protocol specifies a systematic review of empirical studies of support practices and reported consequences in product development teams. Human validation, formal eligibility assessment and formal source-verified synthesis remain to be completed.

## Protocol information

### Summary

This systematic review will examine the contexts in which support for product development teams is provided, the support practices enacted, and consequences reported in connection with that support. Eligible evidence reports are peer-reviewed journal articles presenting qualitative, quantitative or mixed-methods empirical studies, with an identifiable development team, actual support connected to its shared work, and at least one relevant consequence reported in the body. Practitioner noticing, interpretation and reasons for selecting support will be extracted where reported, but are not inclusion requirements.

The scope covers shared work on conceiving, designing, implementing, prototyping or evaluating products, services or software, including workplace teams and educational development projects connected to practice. Consequences may concern shared work, participating members, development resources or progress, and shared outputs; favorable, adverse, mixed and null results qualify. Qualitative material will undergo source-based coding, descriptive grouping and thematic comparison. Quantitative outcomes and associations will be organized separately in narrative tables retaining their designs, measures, estimates and uncertainty. Their relation to the qualitative findings may be examined without converting numerical results into qualitative findings, pooling effects, or counting significant or favorable findings as evidence of effectiveness.

## Review plan

### Type of review

This is a protocol for a systematic review of empirical studies of support for development teams. Qualitative, quantitative and mixed-methods designs are eligible under C1–C4 and the report limits. Qualitative material will be examined through source-based coding, descriptive grouping and thematic comparison. Quantitative outcomes and associations will receive a separate narrative organization by study design, support practice, target, measure and reported connection. No meta-analysis, pooled effect estimate or vote counting by direction or statistical significance is planned. Numerical findings will not be converted into qualitative findings or treated as additional qualitative testimony.

The review may develop bounded explanations through comparison of contexts, support and consequences, but will not require a new general theory or impose a common causal process. The protocol will be reported using PRISMA-P, the search using PRISMA-S, and the completed review using PRISMA 2020, with reasons for inapplicable items. Applicable [ENTREQ reporting items](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-12-181) will guide reporting of the qualitative synthesis component. Methodological appraisal will use design-appropriate tools from the [JBI critical appraisal resources](https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools). GRADE-CERQual will apply only to clearly specified qualitative synthesis findings. It will not rate quantitative results, the combined evidence base or an overarching theoretical model. Kado will trial the relevant instructions and forms before formal use; these trials are planned work, not completed calibration or independent validation.

### Background

Support for teams developing products, services or software may concern the development work itself, collaboration, learning, meetings or ways of working. Reports describe many possible consequences, including changes in participation, understanding and coordination, development progress, resource demands and shared artifacts. Such results can coexist with burdens, unresolved problems or null findings. Understanding what support was provided, in which context and with what reported consequences is distinct from demonstrating that the support caused a benefit.

Existing reviews synthesize [team-coaching evidence](https://doi.org/10.53841/bpsicpr.2020.15.2.54), [internal-coach competencies](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs14060452), [agile-coaching research](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-94544-1_2), [design-facilitation expertise](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2025.101342), [co-design facilitation approaches](https://doi.org/10.1080/15710882.2025.2540926) and [conceptual perspectives on participatory-design facilitation](https://doi.org/10.1080/15710882.2026.2648668). These bodies of work already address context, agency, expertise and situated practice. The present review will compare concrete source-reported connections between actual support and consequences for shared development work, preserving their targets, evidence forms and limits. Comparison with nearby syntheses remains necessary before asserting added value or conceptual novelty.

The intended contribution is an integrated account of development contexts, enacted support practices and reported consequences, with qualitative and quantitative evidence kept identifiable. It will examine what was reported as accomplished, what remained unresolved, whose experience or work was affected, and how far the evidence supports a connection to the specified support. Practitioner noticing, interpretation, reasons and reconsideration may explain a practice where the source reports them, but are not the organizing entry criterion.

This contribution concerns what can be learned from the evidence rather than confirming an earlier model. Preparatory noticing codes, participation comparisons and proposed themes will not define eligibility or be retained as a required structure for the new analysis. The review will assess its contribution through substantive comparisons and their relation to existing explanations; a diagram, a product-development boundary or a new label does not itself establish novelty.

### Primary research question(s)

In what contexts is support for product development teams provided, what support practices are enacted, and what consequences for shared work, participating members and development are reported in connection with that support?

### Secondary research question(s)

1. What development work, shared responsibilities and team contexts are involved, and who provides and receives support? What roles, authority, resources and constraints characterize those relationships?
2. What support practices are actually enacted, for which targets and purposes, and at what times? Where noticing, interpretation, choice reasons or reconsideration are reported, how does the source connect them to the support?
3. What consequences for the supported collective, its shared work, participating members, development resources, progress or outputs are reported? Who reports them, with which measures or empirical materials, at what level and with what uncertainty or qualifications?
4. What source evidence connects each consequence to the same supported team or member, actual support and situation? Does it establish a narrated change, qualitative appraisal, association, comparison or another explicitly reported relationship?
5. What similarities, differences, adverse or mixed consequences, null findings and unresolved questions can be supported within and across studies? Which comparisons are restricted by differences in context, target, design, timing or evidence form?

A body-reported relevant consequence connected to actual support is required under C4. A beneficial result, practitioner noticing, explicit interpretation, a choice rationale and a complete temporal sequence are not required. Comparisons will preserve workplace, educational and community contexts, development activities, support targets, roles, authority and evidence forms.

### Expectations / hypotheses

No directional effect hypotheses are specified. Earlier noticing-based labels and explanations are preparatory material, not fixed codes, expected results or models to confirm. The analysis will examine supporting and contrary material, alternative explanations and differences in what each source treats as an accomplishment or unresolved problem. Context, practice and consequence may overlap in a source account; they will not impose separate stages or a uniform cycle. The number and content of qualitative themes and the organization of quantitative findings will remain responsive to the evidence.

### Dependent variable(s) / outcome(s) / main variables

The main evidence is an empirical consequence for an actual supported development collective, its shared work or a participating member, with a source-reported connection to actual support. Relevant targets include collaboration, participation, communication, coordination, member experience or learning, development resources or progress, and production or quality of a shared artifact. A completed artifact qualifies only where the supported collective actually co-produced it and the report connects that work with support; subsequent use need not have been evaluated. Merely naming a co-design project or reporting that a support activity occurred is insufficient.

Qualitative participant or practitioner appraisals, empirical author summaries, narrated changes, quantitative associations and comparisons may qualify when they concern actual supported practice or experience. Positive, adverse, mixed, null and explicitly reported no-change results qualify. Statistical non-significance does not establish equivalence or zero effect. Individual member experience, collective process, development resources or output, and team performance will remain distinguishable. Study-reported estimates and qualitative attributions do not automatically establish causation or comparative effectiveness.

Intended or expected future benefits, recommendations, participant counts or demographics alone, researcher-generated classifications, learning only by a consultant or facilitator not shown as a supported member, and effects of a finished product or clinical service on customers or patients do not independently establish C4. These may be retained as context where relevant. A supported member's experience during co-development or prototype evaluation will be distinguished from a later customer's benefit from the finished service.

Practitioner noticing and interpretation are optional explanatory material. They will remain distinct from independently measured conditions, shared team properties, accurate diagnosis and causal effects. Neither noticing nor an unreported reason for support will be inferred from an action or outcome alone.

### Independent variable(s) / intervention(s) / treatment(s)

The review will record actual support practices and their reported purpose, timing, target, duration or intensity where available, extent of direction, provider and relationship to the team's shared development work. Internal members, managers, teachers, mentors, coaches, researchers and external facilitators may supply support when a specific function is established. Job titles, personal ability scores or general leadership attributes alone do not establish an enacted support practice.

No pooled intervention effect will be estimated. Source-reported comparisons and associations will retain their designs, measures, comparators, analysis adjustments and uncertainty. Coexistence of support and a consequence in one report is insufficient without the source connection required under C4. Any reported noticing, interpretation or reason for selecting the support will be recorded separately as optional information.

### Additional variable(s) / covariate(s)

We will record setting, country where reported, the product, service or software under development, development activities and shared responsibilities, team composition and continuity, the support provider's role and authority, resources and constraints, study design, participants and perspectives, data collection and analysis, study/report overlap, outcome timing and evidence limitations. Context describing the setting will be distinguished from a condition explicitly used by a practitioner or study author to explain the support or its consequences. The source must establish that explanatory connection; background characteristics alone will not supply a mechanism.

### Grey literature

Eligible primary-evidence reports are peer-reviewed journal articles presenting qualitative, quantitative or mixed-methods empirical studies meeting C1–C4. Empirical findings must be grounded in actual data or identifiable empirical material; a study may collect new data or analyse existing empirical material. A paper that only develops a theoretical or conceptual argument, offers opinion or advice, or synthesizes findings from other studies does not independently supply an eligible empirical study. A report combining a literature review with a separable empirical study will be assessed on that empirical component; the title word review or a provider's review label alone does not determine eligibility. Empirical quantitative research does not require a separable qualitative component to qualify.

Conference papers and proceedings, including peer-reviewed full papers published in chapter form, doctoral and master's theses, books and book chapters, standalone preprints and unreviewed research reports are outside the journal-publication boundary. Protocols without empirical findings, abstract-only reports and non-empirical editorials are also outside the evidence boundary. Lawfully accessible accepted manuscripts of an eligible published journal article may be used; the version used does not create a separate study or make a standalone preprint eligible.

Open indexes and the initial Google Scholar searches may identify theses, books or chapters. Their appearance in a search does not establish report-type eligibility. Retain all saved candidates and actual discovery routes, including the initial thesis-query results; a journal article reporting an empirical study found through those queries is assessed under the same criteria. A search route or repository copy does not by itself determine the type of the published report. No subscription dissertation database or exhaustive repository search is claimed.

No publication-type or peer-review restriction is applied at retrieval. Report-type eligibility is assessed during selection. ### Inclusion and exclusion criteria

A primary-evidence report must meet all of the following criteria:

1. The report presents a qualitative, quantitative or mixed-methods empirical study grounded in actual data or identifiable empirical materials and a traceable analytic procedure. Practitioner self-study may qualify on that basis; reflection or advice alone does not qualify. A separable qualitative component is not required for quantitative research.
2. The setting involves a team whose members share responsibility for interdependent work on conceiving, designing, implementing, prototyping, or evaluating products, services, or software. One or more of these activities may establish the development context; the full lifecycle need not be covered. Workplace teams may be ongoing or project-based. Educational project teams connected to practice may qualify where they share responsibility for such development work or a development deliverable. The source must establish the development context; labels such as agile, innovative, or creative alone are insufficient. The scope is not limited to new-product creation or software development. Settings will remain distinct in extraction and comparison.
3. An identifiable practitioner performs a support function connected to that development team's shared work. Support may address development activities themselves or the team's meetings, collaboration, learning, or ways of working. Job title alone is insufficient. Coaches, mentors, facilitators, Scrum Masters, consultants, teachers, and managers may qualify where the source establishes this connection. Routine line management or teaching without a separable team-support practice is outside the inclusion boundary. Organization-wide agile transformation or general work improvement qualifies only where support for an identifiable eligible development team can be separated from the wider activity. A general organizational setting, improvement activity, or software-related job title does not by itself establish eligibility.
4. The report's body describes at least one relevant empirical consequence for the supported development collective, its shared work or a participating member, in connection with actual support. The team/member, support function, consequence and reported connection must be identifiable in the source. Qualitative appraisals, narrated changes, quantitative associations and comparisons may qualify; adverse, mixed and null results are included. Intended benefits, enactment of support without a consequence, customer/patient benefits alone and disconnected descriptions of support and outcomes do not qualify. Practitioner noticing, interpretation, an explicit choice rationale, a beneficial effect and a complete temporal cycle are not required.
5. The report is a peer-reviewed journal article, meets the language criteria specified here and passes the abstract-availability requirement under [Abstract retrieval and availability](#abstract-retrieval-and-availability). Journal-publication status is checked at title-and-abstract assessment wherever it can be established; the empirical evidence requirement is assessed under C1. Abstract availability is a selection restriction, not a substantive C1–C4 criterion.

The eligible full-text language is English. There is no lower publication-year limit. Each search will cover the source's available period to its actual execution date. A journal article first published online by that date may qualify even if its issue date is later; the online-first date, issue date and version will be retained rather than resolved from a metadata year alone. No open-access publication filter will be used. Lawfully accessible accepted manuscripts or other full-text versions of an eligible report may establish eligibility regardless of the publication's access model.

A complete temporal cycle, practitioner noticing, explicit interpretation, actual change in involvement, before-and-after measurement, team-member testimony, a beneficial outcome, and fit to anticipated themes are not required. A relevant body-level support-linked consequence remains required under C4. Missing interpretations or choice reasons will be recorded rather than inferred; they do not by themselves make an otherwise eligible report ineligible or methodologically poor. Mere continuation will not be interpreted as a deliberate decision to maintain support. An abstract not obtained after the specified abstract-retrieval procedure leads to a separately recorded abstract-availability exclusion. A potentially eligible report whose full text cannot be obtained after the separate full-text retrieval procedure will be recorded as not retrieved or awaiting assessment, as appropriate. Neither outcome establishes that a substantive C1–C4 criterion is unmet. Operational eligibility rules are specified under [Used exclusion criteria](#used-exclusion-criteria) and [Screener instructions](#screener-instructions); the human trial will examine boundary cases.

An eligible study must have at least one evidence report meeting the body-level support-linked team-consequence criterion. A verified peer-reviewed journal companion report describing the methods or context of the same empirical study may be retained for study-level extraction without independently meeting C4. Its connection to the included empirical study and its supporting role must be established. Non-journal reports and papers that only provide theory, opinion or synthesis of other studies may support background, identification or citation navigation, but they will not independently supply eligible empirical findings or be counted as included evidence or study-level extraction companions. Each report's role will be identified. Companion status will not create another independent study or justify combining unrelated events into a sequence. All reports used for an included study will remain traceable, with evidence reports and supporting reports distinguished in the study description and selection accounting.

Context may be established at study or sample level and linked to the relevant account; a product name or development activity need not be repeated in every quotation, and an anonymous team may qualify. Where settings are mixed or poorly specified, a development-oriented research question or the presence of some development teams does not establish the context of every account. Unresolved links will be retained for assessment rather than filled by inference. An empirically grounded recurring-practice or cross-case account may qualify when it identifies actual supported experience and a relevant consequence. A conditional explanation may supply context, but a hypothetical or unexperienced scenario, an instruction alone or an unsupported recommendation will not independently establish an actual support–consequence relationship. Report-level eligibility does not make every passage eligible synthesis material.

### Abstract retrieval and availability

A usable abstract must be available before a report enters AI content screening. A usable abstract is a nonblank abstract field that can be faithfully read or reconstructed and linked to the identified report. Explicit provider placeholders and search-result snippets do not qualify. There is no minimum word count, reporting-quality score or requirement for an abstract to describe C1–C4 in full. Missing information about a substantive criterion remains content uncertainty rather than making the abstract unusable.

First reuse the primary-source exports, saved raw metadata and available local reference metadata. Where no usable abstract has been found, use the report's available native identifiers or DOI for applicable OpenAlex, Crossref or DataCite metadata lookups. The initial recovery uses the finite set of saved and executed lookups, with each requested identifier or title and its actual returned range retained. For later discovery or update rounds, fix the applicable lookup list and returned-result bound before execution. Reuse an already saved response instead of repeating a successful request solely because the same report appears through another route. No routine author contact, full-text acquisition solely to rescue a missing abstract, further initial Scholar requests, bypass of access controls, paid expansion or open-ended retry is required for this abstract procedure.

Retain the route, identifier or title, date, returned record and abstract, raw-response hash, and outcome of each attempt. Established non-journal or non-empirical reports will be proposed for exclusion before full-text retrieval, with traceable source evidence. These proposed exclusions remain part of the human reference validation and exclusion audit. The first two dispositions will proceed to full-text retrieval and human eligibility assessment; they are not inclusion decisions. A proposed `clearly_ineligible` disposition must identify an unequivocally unmet criterion and supporting text in the actual input. Absence of outcome information from an abstract will not by itself establish that the body lacks a relevant support-linked consequence. Practitioner noticing and interpretation are not required. Missing abstracts will remain blank while the specified retrieval procedure is applied; a completed abstract-availability exclusion is a separate selection outcome.

Kado will establish a human reference set, review boundary cases and audit a separate sample of AI-proposed exclusions as specified below. Proposed exclusions will remain provisional until those checks are completed and their findings resolved. Records requiring human resolution because of unusable text, ambiguous identity, unsupported AI output or conflicting evidence will remain pending until assessed. Every report advanced to full text will receive Kado's final, source-based eligibility decision. Methodological appraisal and report-to-study linkage will follow selection.

There will be no active-learning ranking, model-switching sequence, negative-run stopping rule or unscreened remainder authorized by a statistical or heuristic stop. Completion requires a recorded availability disposition for every candidate, a first-pass assessment or explicit human resolution for every record passing that check, completion of the prescribed validation and audit, and resolution of pending cases. AI processing coverage and human assessment coverage will be reported separately.

### Screened fields / blinding

The AI first pass will use the title and complete available abstract, with the bibliographic information needed to identify the report and assess explicit report limits. Search-result snippets will not be substituted for abstracts. Kado may inspect keywords, authors, source, dates and original reports as required. Bibliographic masking is not planned. For the human reference set and exclusion audit, Kado will record his initial assessment before viewing the AI disposition, rationale or supporting text. Prior exposure to a report will be recorded; this masking does not make the same reviewer's judgments independent of his prior knowledge.

### Used exclusion criteria

At title-and-abstract assessment, record `TA_NON_JOURNAL_REPORT` when the identified report is established to be outside journal publication and `TA_NON_EMPIRICAL_REPORT` when it is established to present no empirical study or separable empirical component. The latter includes theory, conceptual argument, opinion, advice and review-only synthesis without such a component. If both apply, use the non-journal reason as the primary reason and retain the other finding. Quantitative, qualitative and mixed-methods empirical reports are assessed under the same C1 empirical requirement; absence of qualitative material does not justify exclusion. Record the actual title/abstract and bibliographic or publisher evidence supporting the finding, its source and date, and whether the disposition is AI-proposed or individually assessed by Kado. The non-journal reason is a report-limit finding; the non-empirical reason records failure of the empirical part of C1. Neither is a full-text exclusion or evidence that later C1–C4 requirements were assessed.

Sparse methods reporting, an absent methodological keyword or an uncertain provider label will not by itself establish that a report is non-empirical. Ambiguous identity, journal status or empirical basis requires Kado's resolution; the record may proceed to human full-text assessment where original material is needed.

After the abstract-availability check, content-based title-and-abstract exclusion requires clear evidence that an eligibility criterion is not met; uncertainty leads to full-text assessment. A report for which the bounded retrieval procedure yields no usable abstract is instead recorded as an abstract-availability exclusion, with its attempts and reason retained separately. Absence of outcome reporting from searchable metadata will not by itself justify exclusion. Full texts will establish whether a relevant support-linked consequence is reported. Noticing, interpretation and choice reasons are optional. At full-text assessment, Kado will record one primary exclusion reason using the ordered rules below, with a source location and explanatory note where content is the basis. This primary reason supports auditable flow counts; it does not assert that every other criterion was met. Not-retrieved reports will be counted separately. Expected themes and the richness of optional choice or response reporting are not exclusion criteria.

For retrieved reports, a clearly unmet report-type, language or publication-date limit takes priority (`FT05_REPORT_LIMIT`), with the specific limit recorded. Absence of empirical findings is assessed under `FT01_NO_EMPIRICAL_DATA`, with the C1 failure distinguished from a report-type failure. Otherwise assess C1–C4 in order and use the first unmet criterion: no eligible empirical data (`FT01_NO_EMPIRICAL_DATA`); no eligible development team or separable team component (`FT02_INELIGIBLE_TEAM`); no identifiable actual support function connected to that team (`FT03_NO_TEAM_SUPPORT`); or no body-reported relevant consequence connected to actual support for that team (`FT04_NO_SUPPORT_LINKED_TEAM_OUTCOME`). Criteria not assessed after a decisive exclusion will be recorded as `not_assessed_after_decisive_exclusion`, rather than assumed satisfied. Uncertain criteria will remain pending until the available original material has been checked. Every included evidence report must have source-based support for all criteria. Confirmed methods/context companions will retain their distinct role without being marked as independently satisfying C4.

An AI-proposed exclusion will retain its criterion, rationale, supporting input span and validation status. It will not be labelled a human exclusion unless Kado has actually assessed that record. AI processing failure, missing required input during an unfinished check, unsupported reasoning and incomplete validation are pending statuses, not content-based exclusion reasons. A completed abstract-availability exclusion follows the separate retrieval rule and will not be represented as an AI finding that C1–C4 are unmet.

### Screener instructions

The AI will assess each record individually against version 1.8 C1–C4 and the explicit report limits. Earlier noticing-based or qualitative-only proposals will be reassessed where affected, without silently converting their old labels into the new decisions. It will first check journal-publication status and whether the report presents an empirical study using the supplied title, abstract and traceable bibliographic or publisher information. It will look for actual data or empirical material and reported analysis or findings, without requiring a particular method word or a complete methods description in the abstract. A report is not accepted merely because it is not labelled a review. Conversely, a literature-review section or review label does not exclude a separable empirical component meeting C1–C4.

A journal-like venue name or unverified provider type alone will not establish journal status or peer review. A conference-related special-issue paper is assessed as the identified journal article where that publication is established; a conference-only version remains ineligible. Ambiguous or conflicting evidence requires Kado's resolution. A confirmed published journal version and its accepted manuscript will remain linked rather than treating the repository location of the manuscript as non-journal publication.

The AI must read the full title and every available abstract rather than screen records only by titles, selected terms or topic clusters. Where an abstract is missing, incomplete or cannot be faithfully reconstructed, that condition will be recorded. The system will neither invent missing text nor combine incompatible reports. A title-only record first follows the abstract-retrieval procedure. If no usable abstract is obtained, it receives the separate availability disposition instead of entering title-only AI content screening. Previously saved title-only assessments remain versioned preparatory proposals.

For every proposed assessment, the system will record the record identifier, proposed disposition, criterion-level findings, reason and supporting words from the supplied text. A criterion that cannot be judged from the input will be marked unclear, not failed. Any support quote must be checked as a literal span of the supplied input; a quote-matching check establishes faithful transcription only, not correct interpretation. Invalid, missing or inconsistent output will be retried with a preserved error record or sent for human resolution. A second AI response will not count as human verification.

Before fixing the formal instructions, Kado will trial the criteria and recording forms on purposefully selected varied title-and-abstract records and retrievable full-text reports, recording the cases used. The trial will examine boundaries, uncertainty, source attribution, retrieval handling and the work required for the complete workflow. Purposeful trial cases will support instruction development; they will not supply a representative performance estimate, serve as an unseen validation sample or automatically become formal decisions. Their formal dispositions will be established under the finalized procedure. Earlier AI proposals based on complete titles but only selectively read abstracts will remain exploratory outputs. They will not satisfy this procedure merely because the literal input text is unchanged: the formal first pass requires assessment of every complete available abstract.

### Screening responsibility and decision rule

Masanori Kado is the sole human reviewer and remains responsible for the criteria, the human reference judgments, the exclusion audit, resolution of problematic records and all final full-text eligibility decisions. The AI supplies the first-pass assessment of all records passing the abstract-availability check; it is not a second human reviewer. Not every AI-proposed title-and-abstract exclusion will receive a separate human reassessment. The use of such exclusions depends on completion of the prespecified validation and audit, with transparent reporting of how many records Kado actually assessed.

The review will examine context, actual support and body-reported consequences for product, service or software development teams. No particular support technique, favorable result, practitioner noticing or anticipated theme is required. Workplace development teams and practice-connected educational development projects remain eligible under C2. An enacted support function and a relevant support-linked consequence are required; proposed support or intended benefits alone do not satisfy C3–C4. Missing optional interpretations, choice reasons or reconsideration will not exclude an otherwise eligible report.

For a record passing the abstract-availability check, a potentially eligible or uncertain title-and-abstract assessment means that the report should proceed to full-text assessment. It does not assert that all criteria are satisfied. Full-text assessment against C1–C4 and English-only full-text eligibility remains a separate human decision; a later full-text exclusion will not retrospectively invalidate an appropriate initial decision to seek full text. Taoka and Saito's final supervisory review will not constitute independent duplicate screening or independent validation of Kado's individual decisions.

### Input preparation and cases requiring human resolution

Source identifiers, report identity, original metadata variants and the reason for selecting a representative screening text will be retained. The AI input will include the usable title and complete abstract lawfully available for the verified report, together with traceable venue, publication type and available peer-review evidence needed to assess the report limits. Conflicting or missing type fields will remain explicit rather than being replaced by an assumed category. Reports excluded after the abstract-availability procedure do not enter this content-screening input. An originally blank field with a recovered abstract candidate remains pending until identity and text are resolved; abstract text will not be fabricated. English metadata alone will not establish English full-text eligibility; Kado will confirm the latter separately.

Missing or unusable titles, non-English, mixed or unclear screening-text language, unresolved identity and conflicting screening text will require Kado's resolution. An AI processing attempt does not resolve these conditions. For records passing the availability check, potentially eligible cases will be forwarded where the available material cannot support a clear content-based exclusion. Prior-knowledge records and records used in preparatory or procedure trials will retain their discovery and exposure history. Earlier trial or AI labels will not be converted automatically into formal human judgments or validation-reference labels.

The same eligibility rules and availability check will apply to primary, supplementary, citation and update candidates. Every record passing that check will receive the same AI-first content assessment. Discovery route will remain traceable. A verified additional source identifier for an already represented report adds provenance rather than another report or another screening decision. Different reports from the same study will remain distinct reports.

### Validation batches

A validation batch is a prespecified, versioned set of deduplicated screening records. The initial batch comprises the prepared records from the initial primary and supplementary searches and prior knowledge that pass the abstract-availability check. N counts only those records; availability exclusions are recorded separately before the validation frame is fixed. Records passing the availability check are not removed from N solely on the basis of an AI-proposed non-journal or non-empirical exclusion. Such AI-proposed exclusions are included in E when not already individually assessed by Kado, and remain subject to the same validation and audit before acceptance. A later batch comprises new records or records with changes that could affect eligibility, identified in one prespecified search-update cycle, supplementary acquisition round or citation-search generation, after applying the same availability check. This includes a previously availability-excluded report for which a matching usable abstract has become available. Each round's scope and cutoff, and the batch's membership, stable identifiers and input versions, will be recorded and fixed before formal AI processing and human reference sampling.

Each batch will use one fixed model and configuration, instruction set and versioned input-preparation procedure. If materially different configurations are planned, their record assignments and separate validation batches will be fixed before processing; validation evidence will not be pooled across them to accept exclusions. Source and recovered-abstract differences among records that pass the availability check will remain traceable and will not by themselves create separate batches. Abstract-availability exclusions remain outside both N and E; their exclusion does not count as a validated AI content judgment. Technical request groups, processing chunks and retries do not create validation batches. Batches will not be split, combined or selectively reassigned in response to AI outputs or human validation findings. An instruction or configuration revision will follow the reassessment and fresh-validation rules below; renaming the affected set as a new batch does not preserve acceptance under a superseded procedure.

### Human reference validation

After the instructions and configuration have been fixed, a simple random sample without replacement of min(100, N) records will be drawn from the validation batch of N records defined above, using sorted stable identifiers and a recorded seed. Kado will assess every sampled title and complete available abstract without first viewing its AI assessment. His reference judgment will be either forward to full text, including uncertainty, or clearly exclude with a source-based reason. The frame, selected identifiers, seed, prior exposure, judgments and comparison with the frozen AI outputs will be retained.

The comparison will report the complete disposition table, the number of human-forward records proposed for AI exclusion, the reasons for disagreements, abstract availability and any unsupported AI rationale. Every human-forward record in this reference set must be retained or forwarded by the finalized AI procedure. Human-uncertain cases count as forward for this comparison. Known and purposefully selected boundary cases will also be checked, but their results will not be pooled with the random sample as a representative performance estimate.

If the random reference sample contains no human-forward record, the batch's remaining AI-proposed exclusions that Kado has not individually assessed will remain provisional. Known or purposefully selected examples, or additional checks chosen after seeing this result, will not by themselves authorize their acceptance. Any further validation used to accept these exclusions will require a prospective written specification of its selection method, sample size or maximum extent, and acceptance rule before it begins. Alternatively, actual individual human decisions may complete a set that Kado assesses in full; this will be reported as human assessment rather than successful sampled AI validation. The same rule applies to fresh reference samples after revision and to later batches.

A reference-set false exclusion will be investigated against the source and instructions. Affected records and reason categories will remain pending while any instruction or configuration revision is made. Revised outputs will retain the original version, and evaluation on the cases used to make the revision will be identified as development checking. Acceptance of the revised procedure will require a fresh random reference sample of the same size, where sufficient unexposed records remain, and the separate exclusion audit below. If an adequate new assessment cannot be completed, AI-proposed exclusions will remain provisional rather than being accepted on the basis of a reused test set. Where Kado has actually assessed every record in a small set, those individual human decisions may complete its assessment; this will be reported as complete human assessment, not validated AI exclusion.

The sample size is a practical operating choice, not a validated guarantee of recall or accuracy. The reference judgments come from one human reviewer; neither these judgments nor agreement with them removes the possibility of shared errors. No validation has yet been completed for the finalized procedure.

### Human audit of AI-proposed exclusions

After reference validation, Kado will independently draw a simple random sample without replacement of min(100, E) remaining AI-proposed exclusions, where E is the number of AI-proposed exclusions in that validation batch not already individually assessed by him. The audit frame, seed and selected identifiers will be recorded. He will make his initial title-and-abstract judgment without first viewing the AI assessment. Any record that he considers potentially eligible or uncertain will proceed to full-text assessment. Such a discrepancy counts as an AI false exclusion for the audit, even if the report is later excluded at full text.

Purposeful boundary checks, including known potentially eligible reports, records with recovered abstracts and varied proposed exclusion reasons, will complement the random samples. Separate checks of abstract-availability and unresolved identity cases will examine the application of that rule without entering the AI audit denominator. Their selection and results will be reported separately rather than pooled as a representative performance estimate. Prior reference and trial cases will not be presented as new independent audit cases. The specified sample sizes are operational choices, not evidence that all sources of error have been covered.

Any false exclusion will be corrected and its cause investigated. All proposed exclusions plausibly affected by that cause will return to pending status and be reprocessed or manually resolved. After any false exclusion has been remedied, a fresh random audit of min(100, E) remaining unassessed proposed exclusions is required, whether or not the instructions or configuration changed. Corrected audit cases are development checks and will not be reused as a new audit sample. A change to instructions or configuration will also require reassessment of the full processed set and fresh human reference validation under the finalized version. If all remaining proposed exclusions have been individually assessed by Kado, their actual human decisions may complete that set; this will be reported as complete human assessment rather than successful sampled AI validation. Agreement among AI outputs will not resolve the error. If the affected scope cannot be established, the entire remaining AI-proposed exclusion set will stay pending. If a fresh audit still identifies unresolved errors, or the required remedial work cannot be completed, AI-proposed exclusions will not be accepted as formal exclusions. The affected work will remain incomplete unless a documented human-assessment route or other prospective method amendment is explicitly adopted; a full repeat human assessment of every record is not an unstated requirement of this procedure.

Completion of the audit requires no unresolved false exclusions in its final assessed version. An audit with no detected false exclusions will be reported as that observation, with its actual sample and denominator; it will not be described as proof of zero missed reports or a numerical recall guarantee. Records will remain recoverable throughout, and no AI disposition will irreversibly delete the underlying record, source text or decision history.

### Citation discovery, updates and corrections

Every later discovery will be linked to its stable identifier and compared with the preserved screening version.

| Later event | Action |
| --- | --- |
| A search supplies the same verified report with unchanged screening text and eligibility-relevant metadata | Retain its valid assessment under the current procedure, add source/query/date provenance and do not count another screening decision. |
| A new report is identified | Apply the abstract-availability check. If it passes, apply the finalized AI first-pass procedure and include the new batch in validation and exclusion auditing before accepting its proposed content exclusions. Record an abstract-availability exclusion separately where applicable. |
| Title, abstract, language, report identity, report type or date changes in a way that could affect eligibility | Preserve both versions and assess the impact. A matching usable abstract obtained after an availability exclusion reopens that disposition. Include records passing the availability check in the relevant later validation batch for reassessment under the current procedure. Unresolved effects remain pending. |
| A human judgment or AI exclusion is corrected, or a merge reveals conflicting assessments | Preserve the original history, resolve the report identity and competing decisions, and investigate whether other records share the error. Reassess the affected set and repeat validation or auditing where the error challenges their basis. |
| An additional source identifier refers to an already represented report, or only citation counts or unrelated metadata change | Update provenance or metadata without inventing a new eligibility decision. |

A later batch, as defined under [Validation batches](#validation-batches), will not inherit a claim of completed validation solely because an earlier batch was checked. Its input and processing conditions will be compared with the validated configuration, and the reference sample and exclusion audit will be repeated within that batch, up to the specified sample sizes. The rule for a reference sample with no human-forward record also applies. A substantive eligibility or method change requires a dated amendment and reassessment of affected records before their decisions are used.

### Completion and limitations

Title-and-abstract selection will be considered complete only when every candidate has a recorded availability disposition, every record passing that check has a preserved AI assessment or a human resolution, the required reference validation and exclusion audit have been completed, errors and affected records have been resolved, and no proposed content exclusion remains pending acceptance. Availability exclusions will be reported separately. Outstanding retrieval or identity cases prevent completion of the affected set and remain explicitly unresolved. There will be no residual set declared excluded because a model has stopped presenting relevant records. Reports forwarded from any route will undergo the specified retrieval and human full-text assessment; unsuccessful retrieval will remain a documented retrieval outcome.

The abstract-availability rule may exclude substantively eligible reports with unavailable or poorly indexed abstracts. The bounded routes do not establish universal online availability. AI processing errors, missing or misleading metadata, insufficient information in titles and abstracts, model bias, shared prompt errors and human misjudgments may also cause missed evidence. Complete processing is not complete human reading, and neither complete processing nor a successful sampled audit establishes a numerical recall guarantee, study-level coverage or representation of every relevant perspective. When the required checks or resolutions cannot be finished, the affected stage will remain incomplete rather than being reported as validated.

### Screening reliability

Kado is the sole human screener. His actual assessments will be identifiable separately from AI-only assessments and proposed exclusions accepted after sampled validation. The random reference sample and the independent draw of proposed exclusions provide review-specific checks on the AI first pass; both are judged by the same human reviewer and do not provide independent duplicate human screening. Purposeful boundary cases, synthetic examples and agreement among models will not be treated as a human reference standard or a representative estimate of performance.

Content uncertainty in records passing the abstract-availability check will be forwarded to full text. Source-based reasons, preservation of original and revised assessments, and targeted investigation of disagreements improve traceability but do not eliminate screening error. Taoka and Saito's supervisory review of final results and manuscript remains distinct from independent assessment of individual records. Prior discussion and exposure will be described accurately.

### Screening reconciliation procedure

There is no planned third adjudicator or inter-reviewer reconciliation procedure. Kado will resolve uncertain cases by rechecking original material and the criteria, documenting alternative readings and the reason for his final decision. AI-human disagreement will be resolved through this source-based human assessment, not majority voting among AI outputs. Supervisory discussion will be identified as such. A systematic ambiguity requiring a material rule change will pause the affected work for a dated amendment and reassessment of earlier affected decisions.

### Sampling and sample size

Every retrieved, deduplicated screening record passing the abstract-availability check will enter the first-pass assessment; there is no sampling among these records for AI screening and no early stopping. Availability exclusions are counted separately and remain outside the AI validation and audit frames. Sampling is used only for the prespecified human reference validation and audit of proposed exclusions. Their actual frames, denominators, seeds, selected identifiers, human judgments, AI versions and all revisions will be retained. Targeted boundary checks will remain distinct from random samples.

All studies found eligible through the completed selection procedure will be considered for synthesis, without thematic or convenience sampling of eligible studies. The validation and audit sample sizes do not establish a numerical recovery threshold, a confidence guarantee or representation of every relevant perspective. Unresolved source comparisons will remain unresolved rather than being filled by inference.

### Screening procedure justification

The procedure uses AI to assess the entire title-and-abstract set remaining after the recorded availability check, and uses human work for reference validation, exclusion auditing, problematic cases and every final full-text decision. It aims to reduce repeated human assessment of clearly irrelevant metadata while retaining content uncertainty for full-text checking. The separate availability restriction reduces the set that can enter this workflow and may lose relevant evidence; it is not a finding of substantive irrelevance. This is an AI first-pass workflow with sampled human checks, not supervised active-learning prioritization or independent duplicate human screening.

The complete workflow, including input preparation, reference judgments, audits, error resolution, retrieval and full-text decisions, must be assessed for feasibility before its completion is claimed. Successful import, valid output formatting, literal quote matching or model agreement does not establish review-specific screening performance. If the prespecified checks cannot support use of the AI exclusions, those exclusions remain provisional; the review will resolve the affected set or report incomplete work. A further material method change will be recorded before the affected formal work.

### Data management and sharing

Screening records will retain source-record, report and study identifiers, discovery routes, exact input versions and hashes, abstract availability, AI service and exposed model identifiers, instructions and configuration versions, output versions, date, disposition, criterion-level reasons, supporting source spans, processing failures and corrections. Human records will additionally retain reviewer, actual assessment date, judgment, reason, source location and prior exposure. Abstract-availability exclusions, proposed title-and-abstract report-limit exclusions, proposed C1 exclusions for absent empirical evidence, other proposed AI content exclusions, acceptance after validation, individual human exclusions, pending cases, report duplicates and full-text retrieval failures will remain distinct statuses. Report-type and empirical-status records will retain the non-journal or non-empirical reason, supporting source and venue evidence, conflicts, reviewer status and any later correction; an AI proposal is not an individual human decision. Availability dispositions will retain the applicable route and attempt evidence, identity or text issues, actual date and later reopening if an abstract is recovered.

Validation and audit records will preserve sampling frames, seeds, selected identifiers, human assessments made before AI inspection, comparison tables, detected errors, affected-set definitions, remedial work and the basis for accepting or declining the resulting exclusion set. Counts will distinguish discovery source rows, deduplicated reports, abstract-availability exclusions and unresolved availability cases, records processed by AI, unique records actually assessed by Kado, repeated human checks, sampled checks, AI-only content exclusions accepted after validation, reports sought, full-text retrieval failures and final human full-text decisions. Overlap with earlier preparatory content proposals will remain traceable without counting a report twice in selection accounting. A repeated check is not another unique screened report, and an AI output is not a human decision.

The review will report the availability of its data and materials and provide supplementary information where required by the applicable reporting guidance or journal. This protocol does not promise publication of all working records, source text, software or instructions. Credentials, private local paths, private reference identifiers, copyrighted abstracts and full text, private correspondence and internal working materials will not be included in public files.

### Miscellaneous screening details

Prior-knowledge records will retain their discovery route and receive assessment under the same substantive criteria. An earlier preparatory or trial label will not become a formal judgment merely by copying it into the current record.

### Entities to extract

The extraction fields and recording instructions are specified here and under Extractor instructions. We will record report and study characteristics; evidence establishing shared development work, interdependence and responsibility; the actual support provider, function and target; empirical materials and analytic method; and the consequence's reporter, target and evidential basis. Support directed at development work and support directed at meetings, collaboration, learning or ways of working may both qualify.

The principal source-finding unit is an empirical account or result connecting context, an actual support practice and a relevant consequence. Each unit will retain its supported collective or member, support action, target and level of consequence, timing, reporter, evidence form, reported direction, uncertainty and source passages. Individual member experience, collective process, development resources/progress or output, and team performance will be recorded separately. Different outcomes from one practice may be separate linked units; splitting will not manufacture independent studies or participants.

The complete body of each candidate report will be examined for relevant consequences irrespective of abstract outcome wording. The current abstract-positive fixed pool is not evidence that other reports lack consequences. A connection requires source evidence for the same supported team or member, actual support and situation at the granularity claimed. Narrated change, retrospective appraisal, association, comparison, unresolved correspondence and simple coexistence will remain distinguishable. Generic empirical practice or cross-case accounts may qualify without inventing one episode, but unexperienced hypotheticals and expected benefits will not satisfy C4.

Qualitative units will retain faithful paraphrases and the shortest passages preserving their meaning, with adjacent context where needed. Quantitative extraction will additionally retain study design, sample and analysis unit, measure and scale direction, comparator or exposure, timing, reported estimates and uncertainty, adjustment, significance thresholds and relevant limitations. Associations, controlled comparisons, before-and-after results, descriptive quantities and resource demands will not be treated as interchangeable. Exact reported values and internal inconsistencies will be preserved rather than silently corrected.

Noticing, interpretation, choice reasons and reconsideration are optional explanatory fields. Where present, a practitioner's perception, participant account, study-author interpretation and review interpretation will remain separately attributed. Background setting will remain distinct from a contextual condition the source explicitly connects to support or a consequence. No action, outcome, shared keyword or nearby passage will supply an unreported perception, rationale or causal link.

Kado will examine methods, design, language, relevant empirical findings and their surrounding context, including decisive tables, figures, vignettes and available supplements. Material may appear in a discussion section if its empirical basis is identifiable; literature summaries, borrowed models and recommendations will remain distinguishable. Entries will retain report and study links, document version and physical-page or section/table/figure locators, source perspective, qualifications and a record of material actually examined. A source excerpt or AI summary is a navigation aid rather than a substitute for source-context checking. An author's model will remain an interpretation, not a new observed consequence.

### Extraction stages

Before formal extraction, Kado will trial the form and AI instructions on a small varied set of eligible-looking reports and revise operational instructions. AI will prepare candidate extraction records from the original material, retaining source passages, locations, attribution and uncertainty. Kado will check every entry used in synthesis against the original material, including its context and qualifications, resolve report-to-study linkage and finalize the extraction. Trial material will be reassessed under the finalized instructions; changes and corrections will be logged. Material changes require a dated amendment before the affected work proceeds.

Preparatory summaries, AI suggestions and proposed codes will remain distinct from the formal source-verified analytical records. An AI output will not establish that Kado has read, checked or adopted its contents.

### Extractor instructions

Reports and independent studies will be kept separate. Multiple reports with overlapping participants or observations will be linked only after checking methods, settings, field dates and samples. Suspected overlap will remain uncertain; title similarity alone will not establish one study. One report may describe several studies, and several reports may contribute to one study without supplying independent evidence.

A source finding is an empirically grounded result or account about an identifiable supported development collective or participating member, the actual support and a relevant consequence connected to it. Context may be established at study or sample level when its link to that result is supported. Distinct teams, people, practices, situations, outcome levels and evidence forms will remain distinguishable. A synthesis finding is a review-level statement developed through comparison of source findings. Finding counts, code counts and internal links do not represent independent study counts, prevalence or effects.

Blank fields mean not yet extracted or checked. `NR` means not reported after examining the relevant available source material; `NA` means structurally inapplicable, with a reason where needed; `UNCLEAR` means examined but ambiguous, conflicting or inseparable. Operational failure or an unread report will remain pending rather than become substantive No or Unknown. An otherwise eligible report may have missing optional noticing or interpretation; a source account without a qualifying consequence may provide context within an eligible study but will not independently satisfy C4. Each entry will record source version, locators, responsible human and actual assessment date, and draft, unresolved or confirmed status. AI candidates and human judgments will remain distinct.

### Extractor masking

Kado will know the review questions, source identities and preparatory materials. No masking or independent duplicate extraction is planned. Supervisory review of the final results will not be reported as a second independent extraction.

### Extraction reliability

AI will prepare structured extraction candidates and identify potentially missing or contradictory source passages. Kado will verify every extraction used in synthesis against the original material, including its context, qualifications and source attribution. He will examine companion reports and possible study overlap and retain unresolved links or competing readings instead of filling gaps by inference.

There will be no independent duplicate extraction or duplicate methodological appraisal. Taoka and Saito's final supervisory review may prompt source rechecks and revisions, but will not be counted as independent confirmation of every row or judgment. AI preparation and Kado's source verification are different contributions. The risk of extraction or interpretive error will be acknowledged, with source chains, alternative readings and correction histories retained.

### Extraction reconciliation procedure

Kado will resolve extraction uncertainties by rechecking original passages and field definitions. Where the source does not decide between readings, he will retain the ambiguity or alternative interpretation. A changed definition will trigger reassessment of affected earlier entries. His final reasoning, date and changes, and any supervisory discussion, will be recorded. Missing relationships will not be completed by inference.

### Extraction procedure justification

The form makes the supported collective or member, actual practice, relevant consequence and reported connection explicit while preserving context and attribution. It separates qualitative accounts from quantitative results and optional explanatory material. This permits comparison without constructing a causal sequence from co-occurring information. Checking every unit used in synthesis against its source and retaining a source trail support a transparent single-reviewer analysis; they do not constitute independent verification.

### Data management and sharing

Source documents will be stored privately, with their versions and links to review records retained. Structured review records will use UTF-8 TSV with documented identifiers and controlled values. Missing, inapplicable, unclear, and unresolved work will be distinct. Shareable data will include permissible metadata, the review lead's original paraphrases where appropriate, source locators, and study links. Copyrighted full text and private records will be excluded. The sharing destination remains to be confirmed.

### Miscellaneous extraction details

The observed or reported unit must support any proposed temporal connection. Fragments from different cases, participants, teams, support practices or times will not be assembled into one sequence. A retrospective appraisal will remain a source attribution rather than a demonstrated causal effect. Recurring-practice and cross-case summaries will remain distinct from specific episodes. Quantitative dependence, repeated measures and overlapping samples will be retained. Actual support, proposed changes, intended benefits, enacted revisions and evaluated consequences will not be conflated.

Practitioner noticing and reconsideration are optional. If recorded, a recollection of an earlier perception will remain distinct from evidence of a later interpretation; retrospective interviewing alone does not establish post hoc justification. Continued practice is not an explicit decision to maintain it without a source statement. The practitioner's perception, recipient's appraisal, direct observation, study-author analysis and review interpretation will remain separately attributed.

### Planned data transformations

Qualitative codes, descriptive groups and higher-order themes will retain their report, study and source-passage links; overlapping memberships may be retained with reasons. Kado will check source-language meaning before using translations. Quantitative results will retain their reported metrics and uncertainty in a separate narrative organization; no effect-size pooling, statistical synthesis, conversion of numbers into qualitative testimony, overall study-quality score or vote counting is planned. A conceptual comparison across evidence streams will identify its source basis and remain distinct from either primary evidence form.

### Missing data

Unreported consequences will be distinguished from body outcomes not yet assessed, substantive ambiguity, and actual null or no-change findings. A lack of outcome wording in an abstract will not establish a negative body assessment. A full readable body with no relevant support-linked consequence may fail C4; operational problems remain pending. Missing estimates, uncertainty or design details will be retained as limitations rather than invented. Optional noticing, interpretation, choice rationale and reconsideration may be absent without affecting inclusion. Linked reports of the same verified study may supply context, but gaps will not be filled from unrelated cases. There is currently no routine author-contact plan.

### Data validation

Scripts will check identifiers, cross-references, missing-value codes, required locators, duplicate records, and reported counts. Kado will verify entries against their sources and check overlapping reports for study-level double counting. Corrections and retraction notices will be checked when a report enters the included set and again before submission.

### Quality assessment

Kado will use design-appropriate checklists from the [JBI critical appraisal resources](https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools). The identified study design will determine the tool, rather than applying the qualitative checklist to every report. Qualitative studies will receive the qualitative research checklist; quantitative components will receive the checklist appropriate to their design. For mixed-methods studies, the relevant components will be appraised using the corresponding tools, with additional narrative consideration of limitations in how the components are integrated. The chosen tool, version, component and item-level evidence will be recorded before judgments are made. Where a design does not fit a checklist well, the mismatch and applicable items will be explained rather than applying the qualitative checklist by default.

Before formal appraisal, Kado will trial the applicable instructions and recording forms on varied designs. AI may locate methods passages and prepare item-level candidates; Kado will check the material and make each judgment. No independent duplicate appraisal is planned. Reasons and source locations will accompany judgments, and inapplicability will remain distinct from unreported information. No numerical total, cutoff exclusion, cross-design ranking by Yes counts or automatic weighting will be used. Methodological limitations, relevance and richness of reported material will remain distinct.

For each finding, Kado will identify which design-specific concerns bear on the claim and examine its dependence on the contributing studies. Concerns about confounding, selection, measurement, missing data, timing or attribution will be applied where relevant to the design and inference. JBI appraisal will inform interpretation and sensitivity checks. For qualitative synthesis findings only, relevant methodological concerns will also inform the methodological-limitations component of CERQual alongside coherence, adequacy and relevance. Quantitative results and an overarching integrated model will not receive CERQual ratings.

### Synthesis plan

AI may propose source-linked codes, descriptive groups, evidence tables and alternative interpretations. Kado will check the relevant original material and retain, revise or reject those suggestions. The analysis will begin with the current outcome-based candidate pool and revised criteria, not with the earlier 872 noticing accounts, their codes or focused comparisons as required categories.

1. Within each study, the lead will establish the development context, actual support, target and level of each consequence, evidence form, timing and source-reported connection. Qualifying results, adverse and null material, unresolved correspondences and contextual material will remain identifiable. Report-to-study linkage and overlapping samples will be checked before cross-study counting.
2. Qualitative empirical material will undergo source-based coding, descriptive grouping and comparison to develop higher-order themes about contexts, enacted support and consequences. Definitions, examples, boundaries, alternative readings and contrary material will accompany each theme. Optional noticing, interpretation and reasons may explain a connection but will not determine membership in the corpus.
3. Quantitative outcomes, comparisons and associations will be organized separately in narrative tables by design, support, target, outcome measure and timing. The tables will retain sample/analysis units, reported estimates, uncertainty, adjustment and limitations. Different scales, resource quantities, outcomes, null findings and statistical thresholds will not be collapsed into a common success measure. No meta-analysis, pooled effect, counts or percentages of favorable/significant results, or conversion of numerical findings into qualitative themes is planned.
4. Bounded cross-study comparison may examine whether apparently similar support addresses comparable tasks, people and contexts and what each source reports as accomplished or unresolved. Qualitative explanations and quantitative patterns may be placed alongside each other in an explicitly labelled comparison, with convergence, tension and non-comparability retained. Such an interpretation will not create a new causal effect, an unreported source connection or an additional independent study. Any explanatory model will retain the evidence and limits of each relationship rather than impose a uniform cycle.

The report will present an organization of development contexts and actual support practices, qualitative synthesis findings and their source-linked comparisons, a separate narrative organization of quantitative results, and an account of the evidence and limitations for each inference. A two-level display may distinguish near-source qualitative codes from higher-order themes; it does not govern the quantitative evidence or itself establish a contribution.

All studies meeting the revised criteria will be considered, with no sampling by favorable result or fit to earlier themes. In an eligible report, material without a consequence may explain context but will not supply an independent outcome finding. Missing optional reasons and perceptions will not be filled, and separate accounts will not be combined into a temporal sequence. A completed shared artifact may be a development output without establishing improved quality, effective support or later customer benefit. Where settings, authority, support targets or evidence forms are insufficiently comparable, results will remain side by side rather than be forced into one theme. Outcome-reporting and coding counts will describe the evidence set, not prevalence or effectiveness.

### Criteria for conclusions / inference criteria

Each finding will state its content and scope, evidence form, contributing studies, supporting and challenging material, comparison basis and source locators. Qualitative appraisals, descriptive quantities, associations and comparative estimates will remain identifiable in the conclusions. An association, participant appraisal or temporal sequence will not become a causal explanation without corresponding design and evidence. Optional noticing will not establish accurate diagnosis, a cause of support selection or a shared team state. Source records, reports, studies, accounts and links will have distinct denominators.

GRADE-CERQual will be used only for clearly specified empirical qualitative synthesis findings, considering methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy and relevance. Kado will check the contributing qualitative material, make and explain the component and overall judgments, and record supporting studies in a summary of qualitative findings. Before formal use he will trial the guidance and form. These judgments remain unperformed. Quantitative findings will not be included as additional qualitative observations to raise CERQual confidence. Quantitative narrative summaries, cross-stream interpretations and an overarching theoretical model will not receive CERQual ratings. No substitute overall certainty grade for those outputs is specified.

Conclusions will answer the revised question with source-supported content and bounded comparisons, stating where evidence is insufficient. A classification or model alone will not establish an empirical finding, comparative effectiveness or a universal support mechanism.

### Synthesist blinding

Kado will know the questions, sources and preparatory materials. No masking is planned. Reflexive memos will record how prior research, preparatory reading and proposed labels may shape attention and interpretation, and how contrary evidence changes the analysis.

### Synthesis reliability

Kado will adopt the formal qualitative codes and themes, quantitative narrative summaries and bounded synthesis interpretations after checking the source material, and will make CERQual judgments for qualitative findings only. AI may prepare evidence tables and suggest alternative readings or contrary material; those outputs remain candidates until checked. He will retain the source chain, compare alternative readings, search for contrary material and examine dependence on individual studies. Taoka and Saito will review the final results and manuscript in their supervisory roles. Their comments and resulting revisions will be recorded, but this will not be reported as independent coding, duplicate synthesis or independent confidence assessment. Single-reviewer interpretation remains a limitation.

[GRADE-CERQual guidance](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0689-2) recommends discussion of each assessment by at least two review-team members. The current arrangement does not meet that recommendation: no finding-level CERQual discussion or boundary-case review has been assigned to another person. If such a contribution is later agreed, the person, scope, materials, discussion and resulting changes will be recorded prospectively. Final manuscript review alone will not be counted as that discussion.

### Synthesis reconciliation procedure

Kado will compare alternative interpretations against the source accounts and review question. He will record whether a difference is resolved by a changed code or narrower claim, retained as a competing interpretation, or left unresolved. Supervisory comments will prompt documented source rechecks where relevant; they will not create an independent adjudication process. Unresolved differences affecting a main claim will be reported and considered in its methodological interpretation and, for a qualitative synthesis finding, its confidence judgment.

### Publication bias analyses

Funnel-plot asymmetry and small-study-effect tests are not planned for this non-pooled review. The review will examine the roles, contexts, sources and perspectives represented, and consider inaccessible reports, English-only full-text eligibility, missing or incorrect language metadata, journal-only and empirical-study limits, public-index coverage, self-report and selective accounts of successful or memorable practice. Excluding conference and other non-journal reports may omit relevant evidence; the restriction will be reported as a scope limitation.

The specified discovery frame, exclusion after bounded abstract retrieval, metadata availability, AI screening errors and single-reviewer judgments are additional selection limitations. Requiring a body-reported consequence may omit studies describing support without reporting its results; it does not limit inclusion to beneficial outcomes. The preparatory abstract-positive outcome pool is a restricted subset and will not establish completed selection or the content of the remaining body-unassessed reports. Abstract-availability exclusions may differ systematically from reports with accessible abstracts; the retained record will describe their routes and available characteristics without claiming to know their unassessed findings. Human validation and exclusion audits may detect errors but do not establish the content or direction of all missed findings or guarantee a numerical recall. Reports identified outside the keyword searches will retain their discovery route and undergo the same specified eligibility process.

### Sensitivity analyses / robustness checks

Kado will examine dependence of each main interpretation on individual studies or overlapping report families, relevant design-specific methodological concerns, practitioner self-study, workplace versus educational/community settings, and specific episodes versus recurring or cross-case appraisals. Qualitative findings and quantitative summaries will be checked within their evidence streams before considering broader interpretations. Quantitative associations will be distinguished from controlled comparisons, adjusted from unadjusted analyses, and prospective from retrospective or temporally ambiguous measurement where available.

The analysis will distinguish member experience, collective process, development outputs/resources and performance, and inspect whether an apparent agreement depends on merging those targets. Consequences reported with optional noticing or choice reasons may be compared with those without them, without treating reporting completeness as a quality score. The analysis will consider adverse and mixed results and non-significant findings without vote counting. Findings supported by the fixed preparatory pool will not be presented as findings from completed assessment of all search candidates.

Discovery routes, AI recommendations and corrections will remain traceable. The lead will examine whether source coverage or AI screening affected the material supporting an interpretation, using the required human validation and exclusion-audit records. These human checks are procedural requirements, not optional sensitivity analyses. Insufficient evidence for a planned check will remain a stated limitation.

### Synthesis procedure justification

The question requires attention to context, actual practice, the target and level of a consequence, and the nature of the reported connection. Qualitative coding and thematic comparison can organize meanings, appraisals and experienced changes. Separate narrative organization preserves what quantitative designs measured and estimated without treating those results as interchangeable with qualitative accounts. Source-linked comparison can identify convergent, conflicting and incomplete evidence while retaining attribution, study dependence and methodological limits. Further conceptual explanation will depend on the resulting evidence; it is not a required universal model.

### Synthesis data management and sharing

The review will retain links from source records to reports, studies, extracted accounts, codes, synthesis findings, and confidence judgments. Tables and counts will be generated from the structured records. Shareable data will preserve this evidence trail while excluding copyrighted source text and private information. The final report will state where the materials are available and any access conditions.

### Miscellaneous synthesis details

No additional synthesis procedures are specified beyond those described above.
