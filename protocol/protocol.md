# Practitioner Noticing, Interpretation and Support Practices in Product Development Teams: A Qualitative Systematic Review Protocol

**Protocol version:** 1.6.
**Protocol date:** 22 October 2026 (Japan Standard Time).

This protocol specifies the planned methods for a qualitative systematic review. Human validation and formal eligibility assessment remain to be completed.

## Protocol information

### Summary

This qualitative systematic review will synthesize empirical accounts of what practitioners notice when supporting product development teams, how they interpret it in context, and how these perceptions and interpretations are connected to support practices. It will examine reported states, changes or events, contextual conditions considered by practitioners, their significance for support, and the support provided or chosen. Accounts without an explicit interpretation or specific support choice will remain eligible; comparisons requiring those links will use the accounts in which they are reported.

The scope covers shared work on conceiving, designing, implementing, prototyping or evaluating products, services or software, including workplace teams and educational development projects connected to practice. Support may concern development activities, meetings, collaboration, learning or ways of working. Thematic synthesis will organize reported noticing and compare its connections with context, interpretation and support within and across studies. Descriptive code groups and higher-order themes will display the findings, with source attribution, contextual scope and evidence limitations retained. Reasons, constraints, responses and reconsideration will be examined where reported.

### Subjects

Group and team research; product management; product and service development; organizational behavior; workplace learning; coaching and mentoring; design education; software engineering.

## Review plan

### Type of review

This is a protocol for a systematic review of qualitative evidence, including separately identifiable qualitative findings from mixed-methods research. The selected synthesis method is thematic synthesis: source-based coding, descriptive grouping and development of higher-order themes through comparison. The review will integrate empirical knowledge about reported noticing, its interpretation in context and its connections to support practices, with bounded comparisons where the evidence supports them. Systematic description and comparison can answer the review question; new theory, a causal explanation or a finding that the same cue is interpreted differently is not a required outcome. No meta-analysis is planned.

The protocol will be reported using PRISMA-P, the search using PRISMA-S, and the completed review using PRISMA 2020, with reasons for inapplicable items. Applicable [ENTREQ reporting items](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-12-181) will additionally guide the explanation of qualitative coding, comparison and appraisal. ENTREQ will supplement PRISMA rather than replace it. The planned conduct methods are thematic synthesis, item-level appraisal using the JBI Checklist for Qualitative Research, and GRADE-CERQual for specified empirical synthesis findings. Kado will trial the instructions and forms before the relevant formal stage. These single-reviewer trials are planned work, not completed calibration or independent validation.

### Background

Practitioners supporting product development teams may attend to the progress of work, the basis for a proposed solution, participation in decisions, or conditions affecting their own support. Their accounts can connect development activities to questions about collaboration and learning. Understanding what they regard as relevant to support is distinct from testing whether their diagnosis is correct or whether an intervention improves performance. This review examines these reported perceptions, their interpretation in context and their connections to support practices in teams jointly developing products, services or software, including practice-connected educational projects.

Existing reviews synthesize [team-coaching evidence](https://doi.org/10.53841/bpsicpr.2020.15.2.54), [internal-coach competencies](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs14060452), [agile-coaching research](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-94544-1_2), [design-facilitation expertise](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2025.101342), [co-design facilitation approaches](https://doi.org/10.1080/15710882.2025.2540926) and [conceptual perspectives on participatory-design facilitation](https://doi.org/10.1080/15710882.2026.2648668). These bodies of work already address context, problem identification, agency and situated judgment. The agile-coaching mapping organizes its main results around research topics, benefits and challenges. The present review aims to retain the concrete correspondence between what practitioners are reported to notice, the contextual conditions they consider, the significance they assign to the situation and their support practices where reported. Full-text comparison with the expertise and co-design reviews remains incomplete; no claim that they omit these correspondences is made.

The intended contribution is an integrated account of what empirical studies establish about practitioner noticing, interpretation in context and connections to support for collective development work. It has three connected parts: a systematic organization of the content of noticing; comparison of reported connections among noticing, contextual conditions, meanings and support practices within and across studies; and an account of the evidence, scope and limitations of each synthesis finding. The review will define themes, show concrete examples and boundaries, and retain similarities, differences and contrary accounts with their contexts. Practitioner meanings will remain distinct from study-author and review-author interpretations.

This contribution concerns what can be learned from the body of evidence, rather than establishing a predetermined theoretical claim. Preparatory interests such as opportunities for shared development judgment may inform a possible theme, but will not define the review's overall conclusion or eligibility. Neither a product-development boundary nor a two-level display establishes novelty by itself. The review will assess its added value through the substantive findings it integrates and comparison with nearby syntheses, without treating conceptual innovation as a mandatory result.

### Primary research question(s)

What states, changes and events are practitioners reported to notice when supporting product development teams, how are they reported to interpret them in context, and how are these perceptions and interpretations connected to their support practices?

### Secondary research question(s)

1. What states, changes and events are reported as noticed, by whom, through what information or contact where specified, and in which development-team and support contexts? What source material establishes attribution to the practitioner?
2. What significance are practitioners reported to assign to what they notice, and what contextual conditions are they reported to consider in interpreting the situation or selecting support? Which contextual descriptions establish only the background setting, and which are linked to practitioner judgment in the source?
3. What support practices or choices are reported, and how does the source connect them with noticing, contextual conditions or interpretation? Where reasons, constraints, team responses or subsequent reconsideration are reported, how do they qualify these connections?
4. What similarities, differences and correspondences can be supported within and across studies? These may include similar noticed states or events associated with different meanings or support practices in different contexts, or different noticed conditions associated with related support concerns. Which elements and connections remain unreported or unclear?

Comparisons will retain workplace and educational settings, development activities, support targets, roles and authority, and evidence forms. Attributed noticing connected to team support remains the substantive inclusion requirement under C4. Explicit interpretation, a contextual condition used in judgment, a specific support choice and the later links in question 3 remain optional. No account must supply a complete sequence to contribute to the review.

### Expectations / hypotheses

No directional effect hypotheses are specified. The preparatory work introduced possible labels and explanatory ideas, including how practitioners frame support concerns and whether teams can jointly examine development judgments. These are sensitizing interests, not fixed codes, expected results or a model to confirm. Joint examination of development judgments is one possible theme, not a predetermined central contribution. Kado will revise interpretations through source-based comparison, alternative readings and contrary cases. Noticed content, context, interpretation and support practice may overlap in an account; these interests will not impose separate stages or a fixed sequence. The number and content of themes are open.

### Dependent variable(s) / outcome(s) / main variables

The main evidence is qualitative empirical material identifying what a practitioner is reported to perceive in connection with support for an eligible development team. Its reported significance, contextual grounds and connection to support practices will be extracted where available. Noticing may concern the team, its work, the practitioner or surrounding conditions. Attributed perception and its support connection are required; explicit meaning, a subsequent choice, reasons, a response and an outcome are optional.

Perceptions will remain distinct from independently observed conditions, accurate diagnosis, shared team properties and effects of support. Noticing will not be inferred from an action alone, researcher-only measurement, or disconnected descriptions of a team condition and a support role.

Noticing may already be evaluative, such as a practitioner describing a team as lacking initiative. We will retain the content as reported. Meaning refers to any reported significance for the support concern, possibility or need. Where these cannot be separated in the source, both fields will link to the same meaning unit and the overlap will be recorded; an unreported neutral observation will not be supplied. Empirically grounded explanations of conditions a practitioner attends to in support may qualify alongside accounts of particular events. Reports of an experienced perception will remain distinct from explanations of what the practitioner attends to conditionally; a stated condition will not become an event assumed to have occurred.

### Independent variable(s) / intervention(s) / treatment(s)

No intervention effect is being estimated. The review will retain the perceived condition and any reported purpose, constraint or support practice connected to it. Where a practice is described, we will record what support is provided or chosen, its timing, target, extent of direction and relationship to the team's work, and any source-reported connection to noticing, contextual conditions or interpretation. These are descriptive and comparative fields, not fixed causal variables. The presence or absence of a practice in a report will not supply an unreported perception.

### Additional variable(s) / covariate(s)

We will record setting, country where reported, the product, service, or software being developed, the development activities and shared responsibilities that establish the team context, team composition and continuity, practitioner role and relationship to the team, authority and responsibility, the connection between the support and the team's work, research design, participants and perspectives, data collection, analytic approach, study/report overlap, and the form and limitations of the evidence. These background characteristics will be distinguished from contextual conditions that the practitioner is reported to consider when interpreting a situation or selecting support. The source must establish the latter connection; a description of the setting alone does not show that it entered practitioner judgment. Unreported characteristics and connections will remain unreported.

### Grey literature

Eligible primary-evidence reports are peer-reviewed journal articles presenting empirical qualitative findings or a separable qualitative component of a mixed-methods study under C1–C4. Empirical findings must be grounded in actual data or identifiable empirical material; a study may collect new data or analyse existing empirical material. A paper that only develops a theoretical or conceptual argument, offers opinion or advice, or synthesizes findings from other studies does not independently supply an eligible empirical study. A report combining a literature review with a separable empirical study will be assessed on that empirical component; the title word review or a provider's review label alone does not determine eligibility. Empirical quantitative research without a separable qualitative component remains outside C1, but will not be mislabelled as non-empirical.

Conference papers and proceedings, including peer-reviewed full papers published in chapter form, doctoral and master's theses, books and book chapters, standalone preprints and unreviewed research reports are outside the journal-publication boundary. Protocols without empirical findings, abstract-only reports and non-empirical editorials are also outside the evidence boundary. Lawfully accessible accepted manuscripts of an eligible published journal article may be used; the version used does not create a separate study or make a standalone preprint eligible.

Open indexes and the initial Google Scholar searches may identify theses, books or chapters. Their appearance in a search does not establish report-type eligibility. ### Inclusion and exclusion criteria

A primary-evidence report must meet all of the following criteria:

1. The report presents empirical qualitative findings or a separable qualitative component. Practitioner self-study may qualify when its empirical materials and analytic procedure are traceable; reflection or advice alone does not qualify.
2. The setting involves a team whose members share responsibility for interdependent work on conceiving, designing, implementing, prototyping, or evaluating products, services, or software. One or more of these activities may establish the development context; the full lifecycle need not be covered. Workplace teams may be ongoing or project-based. Educational project teams connected to practice may qualify where they share responsibility for such development work or a development deliverable. The source must establish the development context; labels such as agile, innovative, or creative alone are insufficient. The scope is not limited to new-product creation or software development. Settings will remain distinct in extraction and comparison.
3. An identifiable practitioner performs a support function connected to that development team's shared work. Support may address development activities themselves or the team's meetings, collaboration, learning, or ways of working. Job title alone is insufficient. Coaches, mentors, facilitators, Scrum Masters, consultants, teachers, and managers may qualify where the source establishes this connection. Routine line management or teaching without a separable team-support practice is outside the inclusion boundary. Organization-wide agile transformation or general work improvement qualifies only where support for an identifiable eligible development team can be separated from the wider activity. A general organizational setting, improvement activity, or software-related job title does not by itself establish eligibility.
4. Empirical qualitative material identifies at least one state, change, or event that a practitioner or practitioner group is reported to perceive in connection with support for the eligible development team. This attribution and the connection to support must be traceable to the source; a participant quotation is not mandatory if the study's empirical analysis supplies them. An episode, a grounded account of noticing, a conditional practice account, or a cross-case comparison may qualify. An explicit interpretation, specific choice, choice rationale, response, or revision is not required. Researcher-only measurement or diagnosis of a team change, a role list, a general adaptability claim, advice without an empirical basis, and disconnected descriptions of a team condition and a support role do not qualify. We will not infer noticing from the support action alone.
5. The report is a peer-reviewed journal article, meets the language criteria specified here and passes the abstract-availability requirement under [Abstract retrieval and availability](#abstract-retrieval-and-availability). Journal-publication status is checked at title-and-abstract assessment wherever it can be established; the empirical and qualitative evidence requirements are assessed under C1. Abstract availability is a selection restriction, not a substantive C1–C4 criterion.

The eligible full-text language is English. There is no lower publication-year limit. Each search will cover the source's available period to its actual execution date. A journal article first published online by that date may qualify even if its issue date is later; the online-first date, issue date and version will be retained rather than resolved from a metadata year alone. No open-access publication filter will be used. Lawfully accessible accepted manuscripts or other full-text versions of an eligible report may establish eligibility regardless of the publication's access model.

A complete temporal cycle, actual change in involvement, before-and-after measurement, team-member testimony, a beneficial outcome, and fit to anticipated themes are not required. Missing interpretations or choice reasons will be recorded rather than inferred; they do not by themselves make an otherwise eligible report ineligible or methodologically poor. Mere continuation will not be interpreted as a deliberate decision to maintain support. An abstract not obtained after the specified abstract-retrieval procedure leads to a separately recorded abstract-availability exclusion. A potentially eligible report whose full text cannot be obtained after the separate full-text retrieval procedure will be recorded as not retrieved or awaiting assessment, as appropriate. Neither outcome establishes that a substantive C1–C4 criterion is unmet. Operational eligibility rules are specified under [Used exclusion criteria](#used-exclusion-criteria) and [Screener instructions](#screener-instructions); the human trial will examine boundary cases.

An eligible study must have at least one evidence report meeting the support-linked practitioner-noticing criterion. A verified peer-reviewed journal companion report describing the methods or context of the same empirical study may be retained for study-level extraction without independently meeting C4. Its connection to the included empirical study and its supporting role must be established. Non-journal reports and papers that only provide theory, opinion or synthesis of other studies may support background, identification or citation navigation, but they will not independently supply eligible empirical findings or be counted as included evidence or study-level extraction companions. Each report's role will be identified. Companion status will not create another independent study or justify combining unrelated events into a sequence. All reports used for an included study will remain traceable, with evidence reports and supporting reports distinguished in the study description and selection accounting.

Context may be established at study or sample level and linked to the relevant account; a product name or development activity need not be repeated in every quotation, and an anonymous team may qualify. Where settings are mixed or poorly specified, a development-oriented research question or the presence of some development teams does not establish the context of every account. Unresolved links will be retained for assessment rather than filled by inference. A conditional account may qualify when the empirical report grounds it as the practitioner's account of cues used in supporting the relevant teams. Its condition will not be treated as an event that occurred. A hypothetical scenario explicitly described as unexperienced, an instruction alone or an unsupported recommendation will not independently establish experienced noticing. Report-level eligibility does not make every passage eligible synthesis material.

### Search strategy justification

The strategy makes support-role, team and development wording explicit in searchable metadata. A role-based route is retained without requiring a development block in every route. Managerial or facilitative support does not require a coaching job title. Noticing and interpretation are not mandatory search words because the qualifying account may appear only in the original report.

OpenAlex and public ERIC support complete retrieval of the eight primary expressions through publicly accessible interfaces. Google Scholar provides bounded general discovery through SerpApi. The saved initial thesis-query observations remain historical identification evidence under the current report-type boundary. Candidates from the completed bounded Consensus identification search, explicit prior-knowledge routes and the specified citation generation also enter the same eligibility process. Together these routes define the current discovery frame. Every report found eligible through the confirmed selection procedure remains eligible for synthesis without thematic or convenience sampling.

The public-source strategy does not establish equivalent coverage of subscription indexes or every publisher collection. Bounded supplementary ranking can miss relevant low-ranked reports, and sparse metadata or incorrect language codes can cause further omissions. English-only full-text eligibility and the abstract-availability requirement limit the evidence base. Failure to obtain an abstract through the specified routes does not establish that the report lacks one or that its substantive content is irrelevant. Citation discovery and human investigation of unresolved cases provide further opportunities for identification without proving that coverage losses have been eliminated.

The two-level display of descriptive code groups and higher-order noun-phrase themes is a synthesis method, not a search filter, predetermined result or additional eligibility criterion. Single-reviewer decisions, AI screening error, source coverage, metadata availability and language limits will be reported as distinct limitations. Complete intended retrieval or an explicit amendment, report identity and input checks, human validation, exclusion audits and assessment of the complete workload are prerequisites to claiming completion of the affected procedure.

### Abstract retrieval and availability

A usable abstract must be available before a report enters AI content screening. A usable abstract is a nonblank abstract field that can be faithfully read or reconstructed and linked to the identified report. Explicit provider placeholders and search-result snippets do not qualify. There is no minimum word count, reporting-quality score or requirement for an abstract to describe C1–C4 in full. Missing information about a substantive criterion remains content uncertainty rather than making the abstract unusable.

First reuse the primary-source exports, saved raw metadata and available local reference metadata. Where no usable abstract has been found, use the report's available native identifiers or DOI for applicable OpenAlex, Crossref or DataCite metadata lookups. Where identifiers do not locate a matching field, a bounded exact-title lookup in OpenAlex may be used. The initial recovery uses the finite set of saved and executed lookups, with each requested identifier or title and its actual returned range retained. For later discovery or update rounds, fix the applicable lookup list and returned-result bound before execution. Reuse an already saved response instead of repeating a successful request solely because the same report appears through another route. No routine author contact, full-text acquisition solely to rescue a missing abstract, further initial Scholar requests, bypass of access controls, paid expansion or open-ended retry is required for this abstract procedure.

Retain the route, identifier or title, date, returned record and abstract, raw-response hash, and outcome of each attempt. Preserve failures, nonmatching results, unresolved associations and truncated responses without treating them as a successful exhaustive lookup. An interrupted prescribed attempt remains unresolved rather than being recorded as a completed negative check. If the recorded finite procedure yields no usable abstract, record an abstract-availability exclusion. This means that no usable abstract was obtained through these routes, not that none exists anywhere online. Retrieval limitations and the number of exclusions they affect will be reported.

An empty original source field is not grounds for exclusion when a recovered abstract candidate exists. Resolve its report identity and any conflicting text before selecting the screening input. A recovered candidate with unresolved identity or completeness remains pending; it is not automatically accepted as the original abstract or discarded as unavailable. ### Used exclusion criteria

At title-and-abstract assessment, record `TA_NON_JOURNAL_REPORT` when the identified report is established to be outside journal publication and `TA_NON_EMPIRICAL_REPORT` when it is established to present no empirical study or separable empirical component. The latter includes theory, conceptual argument, opinion, advice and review-only synthesis without such a component. If both apply, use the non-journal reason as the primary reason and retain the other finding. Empirical quantitative reports without eligible qualitative material are assessed under C1 rather than called non-empirical. Record the actual title/abstract and bibliographic or publisher evidence supporting the finding, its source and date, and whether the disposition is AI-proposed or individually assessed by Kado. The non-journal reason is a report-limit finding; the non-empirical reason records failure of the empirical part of C1. Neither is a full-text exclusion or evidence that later C1–C4 requirements were assessed.

Sparse methods reporting, an absent methodological keyword or an uncertain provider label will not by itself establish that a report is non-empirical. Ambiguous identity, journal status or empirical basis requires Kado's resolution; the record may proceed to human full-text assessment where original material is needed.

After the abstract-availability check, content-based title-and-abstract exclusion requires clear evidence that an eligibility criterion is not met; uncertainty leads to full-text assessment. A report for which the bounded retrieval procedure yields no usable abstract is instead recorded as an abstract-availability exclusion, with its attempts and reason retained separately. Absence of noticing, interpretation or choice reasons from searchable metadata will not by itself justify exclusion. Full texts will establish whether support-linked practitioner noticing is present. At full-text assessment, Kado will record one primary exclusion reason using the ordered rules below, with a source location and explanatory note where content is the basis. This primary reason supports auditable flow counts; it does not assert that every other criterion was met. Not-retrieved reports will be counted separately. Expected themes and the richness of optional choice or response reporting are not exclusion criteria.

For retrieved reports, a clearly unmet report-type, language or publication-date limit takes priority (`FT05_REPORT_LIMIT`), with the specific limit recorded. Absence of empirical findings or an eligible qualitative component is assessed under `FT01_NO_EMPIRICAL_QUAL_DATA`, with the specific C1 failure distinguished, rather than as a report-type failure. Otherwise assess C1–C4 in order and use the first unmet criterion: no eligible empirical qualitative material (`FT01_NO_EMPIRICAL_QUAL_DATA`); no eligible development team or separable team component (`FT02_INELIGIBLE_TEAM`); no identifiable support function connected to that team (`FT03_NO_TEAM_SUPPORT`); or no practitioner-attributed noticing connected to that support (`FT04_NO_SUPPORT_LINKED_NOTICING`). Criteria not assessed after a decisive exclusion will be recorded as `not_assessed_after_decisive_exclusion`, rather than assumed satisfied. Uncertain criteria will remain pending until the available original material has been checked. Every included evidence report must have source-based support for all criteria. Confirmed methods/context companions will retain their distinct role without being marked as independently satisfying C4.

An AI-proposed exclusion will retain its criterion, rationale, supporting input span and validation status. It will not be labelled a human exclusion unless Kado has actually assessed that record. AI processing failure, missing required input during an unfinished check, unsupported reasoning and incomplete validation are pending statuses, not content-based exclusion reasons. A completed abstract-availability exclusion follows the separate retrieval rule and will not be represented as an AI finding that C1–C4 are unmet.

### Screener instructions

The substantive C1–C4 criteria remain unchanged. The AI will assess each record individually against those criteria and the explicit report limits. It will first check journal-publication status and whether the report presents an empirical study using the supplied title, abstract and traceable bibliographic or publisher information. It will look for actual data or empirical material and reported analysis or findings, without requiring a particular method word or a complete methods description in the abstract. A report is not accepted merely because it is not labelled a review. Conversely, a literature-review section or review label does not exclude a separable empirical component meeting C1–C4.

A journal-like venue name or unverified provider type alone will not establish journal status or peer review. A conference-related special-issue paper is assessed as the identified journal article where that publication is established; a conference-only version remains ineligible. Ambiguous or conflicting evidence requires Kado's resolution. A confirmed published journal version and its accepted manuscript will remain linked rather than treating the repository location of the manuscript as non-journal publication.

The AI must read the full title and every available abstract rather than screen records only by titles, selected terms or topic clusters. Where an abstract is missing, incomplete or cannot be faithfully reconstructed, that condition will be recorded. The system will neither invent missing text nor combine incompatible reports. A title-only record first follows the abstract-retrieval procedure. If no usable abstract is obtained, it receives the separate availability disposition instead of entering title-only AI content screening. Previously saved title-only assessments remain versioned preparatory proposals.

For every proposed assessment, the system will record the record identifier, proposed disposition, criterion-level findings, reason and supporting words from the supplied text. A criterion that cannot be judged from the input will be marked unclear, not failed. Any support quote must be checked as a literal span of the supplied input; a quote-matching check establishes faithful transcription only, not correct interpretation. Invalid, missing or inconsistent output will be retried with a preserved error record or sent for human resolution. A second AI response will not count as human verification.

Before fixing the formal instructions, Kado will trial the criteria and recording forms on purposefully selected varied title-and-abstract records and retrievable full-text reports, recording the cases used. The trial will examine boundaries, uncertainty, source attribution, retrieval handling and the work required for the complete workflow. Purposeful trial cases will support instruction development; they will not supply a representative performance estimate, serve as an unseen validation sample or automatically become formal decisions. Their formal dispositions will be established under the finalized procedure. Earlier AI proposals based on complete titles but only selectively read abstracts will remain exploratory outputs. They will not satisfy this procedure merely because the literal input text is unchanged: the formal first pass requires assessment of every complete available abstract.

### Screening responsibility and decision rule

Masanori Kado is the sole human reviewer and remains responsible for the criteria, the human reference judgments, the exclusion audit, resolution of problematic records and all final full-text eligibility decisions. The AI supplies the first-pass assessment of all records passing the abstract-availability check; it is not a second human reviewer. Not every AI-proposed title-and-abstract exclusion will receive a separate human reassessment. The use of such exclusions depends on completion of the prespecified validation and audit, with transparent reporting of how many records Kado actually assessed.

The review will continue to examine what practitioners are reported to notice, and how they are reported to interpret those observations, while supporting the shared work of product, service or software development teams. No particular support activity or substantive theme will become a prerequisite for inclusion. Workplace development teams and practice-connected educational development projects remain eligible under the existing criteria. Meaning, subsequent action, success and a complete support cycle remain optional reported information rather than new inclusion requirements.

For a record passing the abstract-availability check, a potentially eligible or uncertain title-and-abstract assessment means that the report should proceed to full-text assessment. It does not assert that all criteria are satisfied. Full-text assessment against C1–C4 and English-only full-text eligibility remains a separate human decision; a later full-text exclusion will not retrospectively invalidate an appropriate initial decision to seek full text. Taoka and Saito's final supervisory review will not constitute independent duplicate screening or independent validation of Kado's individual decisions.

### Input preparation and cases requiring human resolution

Source identifiers, report identity, original metadata variants and the reason for selecting a representative screening text will be retained. The AI input will include the usable title and complete abstract lawfully available for the verified report, together with traceable venue, publication type and available peer-review evidence needed to assess the report limits. Conflicting or missing type fields will remain explicit rather than being replaced by an assumed category. Reports excluded after the abstract-availability procedure do not enter this content-screening input. An originally blank field with a recovered abstract candidate remains pending until identity and text are resolved; abstract text will not be fabricated. English metadata alone will not establish English full-text eligibility; Kado will confirm the latter separately.

Missing or unusable titles, non-English, mixed or unclear screening-text language, unresolved identity and conflicting screening text will require Kado's resolution. An AI processing attempt does not resolve these conditions. For records passing the availability check, potentially eligible cases will be forwarded where the available material cannot support a clear content-based exclusion. Prior-knowledge records and records used in preparatory or procedure trials will retain their discovery and exposure history. Earlier trial or AI labels will not be converted automatically into formal human judgments or validation-reference labels.

The same eligibility rules and availability check will apply to primary, supplementary, citation and update candidates. Every record passing that check will receive the same AI-first content assessment. Discovery route will remain traceable. A verified additional source identifier for an already represented report adds provenance rather than another report or another screening decision. Different reports from the same study will remain distinct reports.

### Validation batches

A validation batch is a prespecified, versioned set of deduplicated screening records. The initial batch comprises the prepared records from the initial primary and supplementary searches and prior knowledge that pass the abstract-availability check. N counts only those records; availability exclusions are recorded separately before the validation frame is fixed. Records passing the availability check are not removed from N solely on the basis of an AI-proposed non-journal or non-empirical exclusion. Such AI-proposed exclusions are included in E when not already individually assessed by Kado, and remain subject to the same validation and audit before acceptance. A later batch comprises new records or records with changes that could affect eligibility, identified in one prespecified search-update cycle, supplementary acquisition round or citation-search generation, after applying the same availability check. This includes a previously availability-excluded report for which a matching usable abstract has become available. Each round's scope and cutoff, and the batch's membership, stable identifiers and input versions, will be recorded and fixed before formal AI processing and human reference sampling.

Each batch will use one fixed model and configuration, instruction set and versioned input-preparation procedure. If materially different configurations are planned, their record assignments and separate validation batches will be fixed before processing; validation evidence will not be pooled across them to accept exclusions. Source and recovered-abstract differences among records that pass the availability check will remain traceable and will not by themselves create separate batches. Abstract-availability exclusions remain outside both N and E; their exclusion does not count as a validated AI content judgment. Technical request groups, processing chunks and retries do not create validation batches. Batches will not be split, combined or selectively reassigned in response to AI outputs or human validation findings. An instruction or configuration revision will follow the reassessment and fresh-validation rules below; renaming the affected set as a new batch does not preserve acceptance under a superseded procedure.

### Human reference validation

After the instructions and configuration have been fixed, a simple random sample without replacement of min(100, N) records will be drawn from the validation batch of N records defined above, using sorted stable identifiers and a recorded seed. Kado will assess every sampled title and complete available abstract without first viewing its AI assessment. His reference judgment will be either forward to full text, including uncertainty, or clearly exclude with a source-based reason. The frame, selected identifiers, seed, prior exposure, judgments and comparison with the frozen AI outputs will be retained.

The comparison will report the complete disposition table, the number of human-forward records proposed for AI exclusion, the reasons for disagreements, abstract availability and any unsupported AI rationale. Every human-forward record in this reference set must be retained or forwarded by the finalized AI procedure. Human-uncertain cases count as forward for this comparison. Known and purposefully selected boundary cases will also be checked, but their results will not be pooled with the random sample as a representative performance estimate.

If the random reference sample contains no human-forward record, the batch's remaining AI-proposed exclusions that Kado has not individually assessed will remain provisional. Known or purposefully selected examples, or additional checks chosen after seeing this result, will not by themselves authorize their acceptance. Any further validation used to accept these exclusions will require a prospective written specification of its selection method, sample size or maximum extent, and acceptance rule before it begins. Alternatively, actual individual human decisions may complete a set that Kado assesses in full; this will be reported as human assessment rather than successful sampled AI validation. The same rule applies to fresh reference samples after revision and to later batches.

A reference-set false exclusion will be investigated against the source and instructions. Affected records and reason categories will remain pending while any instruction or configuration revision is made. Revised outputs will retain the original version, and evaluation on the cases used to make the revision will be identified as development checking. Acceptance of the revised procedure will require a fresh random reference sample of the same size, where sufficient unexposed records remain, and the separate exclusion audit below. If an adequate new assessment cannot be completed, AI-proposed exclusions will remain provisional rather than being accepted on the basis of a reused test set. Where Kado has actually assessed every record in a small set, those individual human decisions may complete its assessment; this will be reported as complete human assessment, not validated AI exclusion.

The sample size is a practical operating choice, not a validated guarantee of recall or accuracy. The reference judgments come from one human reviewer; neither these judgments nor agreement with them removes the possibility of shared errors. No validation has yet been completed for the finalized procedure.

### Human audit of AI-proposed exclusions

After reference validation, Kado will independently draw a simple random sample without replacement of min(100, E) remaining AI-proposed exclusions, where E is the number of AI-proposed exclusions in that validation batch not already individually assessed by him. The audit frame, seed and selected identifiers will be recorded. He will make his initial title-and-abstract judgment without first viewing the AI assessment. Any record that he considers potentially eligible or uncertain will proceed to full-text assessment. Such a discrepancy counts as an AI false exclusion for the audit, even if the report is later excluded at full text.

Purposeful boundary checks, including known potentially eligible reports, records with recovered abstracts and varied proposed exclusion reasons, will complement the random samples. Separate checks of abstract-availability and unresolved identity cases will examine the application of that rule without entering the AI audit denominator. Their selection and results will be reported separately rather than pooled as a representative performance estimate. Prior reference and trial cases will not be presented as new independent audit cases. The specified sample sizes are operational choices, not evidence that all sources of error have been covered.

Any false exclusion will be corrected and its cause investigated. All proposed exclusions plausibly affected by that cause will return to pending status and be reprocessed or manually resolved. After any false exclusion has been remedied, a fresh random audit of min(100, E) remaining unassessed proposed exclusions is required, whether or not the instructions or configuration changed. Corrected audit cases are development checks and will not be reused as a new audit sample. A change to instructions or configuration will also require reassessment of the full processed set and fresh human reference validation under the finalized version. If all remaining proposed exclusions have been individually assessed by Kado, their actual human decisions may complete that set; this will be reported as complete human assessment rather than successful sampled AI validation. Agreement among AI outputs will not resolve the error. If the affected scope cannot be established, the entire remaining AI-proposed exclusion set will stay pending. If a fresh audit still identifies unresolved errors, or the required remedial work cannot be completed, AI-proposed exclusions will not be accepted as formal exclusions. The affected work will remain incomplete unless a documented human-assessment route or other prospective method amendment is explicitly adopted; a full repeat human assessment of every record is not an unstated requirement of this procedure.

Completion of the audit requires no unresolved false exclusions in its final assessed version. An audit with no detected false exclusions will be reported as that observation, with its actual sample and denominator; it will not be described as proof of zero missed reports or a numerical recall guarantee. Records will remain recoverable throughout, and no AI disposition will irreversibly delete the underlying record, source text or decision history.

### Citation discovery, updates and corrections

Every later discovery will be linked to its stable identifier and compared with the preserved screening version.

| Later event | Action |
| --- | --- |
| A search supplies the same verified report with unchanged screening text and eligibility-relevant metadata | Retain its valid assessment under the current procedure, add source/query/date provenance and do not count another screening decision. |
| A new report is identified | Apply the abstract-availability check. If it passes, apply the finalized AI first-pass procedure and include the new batch in validation and exclusion auditing before accepting its proposed content exclusions. Record an abstract-availability exclusion separately where applicable. |
| Title, abstract, language, report identity, report type or date changes in a way that could affect eligibility | Preserve both versions and assess the impact. A matching usable abstract obtained after an availability exclusion reopens that disposition. Include records passing the availability check in the relevant later validation batch for reassessment under the current procedure. Unresolved effects remain pending. |
| A human judgment or AI exclusion is corrected, or a merge reveals conflicting assessments | Preserve the original history, resolve the report identity and competing decisions, and investigate whether other records share the error. Reassess the affected set and repeat validation or auditing where the error challenges their basis. |
| An additional source identifier refers to an already represented report, or only citation counts or unrelated metadata change | Update provenance or metadata without inventing a new eligibility decision. |

A later batch, as defined under [Validation batches](#validation-batches), will not inherit a claim of completed validation solely because an earlier batch was checked. Its input and processing conditions will be compared with the validated configuration, and the reference sample and exclusion audit will be repeated within that batch, up to the specified sample sizes. The rule for a reference sample with no human-forward record also applies. A substantive eligibility or method change requires a dated amendment and reassessment of affected records before their decisions are used.

### Completion and limitations

Title-and-abstract selection will be considered complete only when every candidate has a recorded availability disposition, every record passing that check has a preserved AI assessment or a human resolution, the required reference validation and exclusion audit have been completed, errors and affected records have been resolved, and no proposed content exclusion remains pending acceptance. Availability exclusions will be reported separately. Outstanding retrieval or identity cases prevent completion of the affected set and remain explicitly unresolved. There will be no residual set declared excluded because a model has stopped presenting relevant records. Reports forwarded from any route will undergo the specified retrieval and human full-text assessment; unsuccessful retrieval will remain a documented retrieval outcome.

The abstract-availability rule may exclude substantively eligible reports with unavailable or poorly indexed abstracts. The bounded routes do not establish universal online availability. AI processing errors, missing or misleading metadata, insufficient information in titles and abstracts, model bias, shared prompt errors and human misjudgments may also cause missed evidence. Complete processing is not complete human reading, and neither complete processing nor a successful sampled audit establishes a numerical recall guarantee, study-level coverage or representation of every qualitative perspective. When the required checks or resolutions cannot be finished, the affected stage will remain incomplete rather than being reported as validated.

### Screening reliability

Kado is the sole human screener. His actual assessments will be identifiable separately from AI-only assessments and proposed exclusions accepted after sampled validation. The random reference sample and the independent draw of proposed exclusions provide review-specific checks on the AI first pass; both are judged by the same human reviewer and do not provide independent duplicate human screening. Purposeful boundary cases, synthetic examples and agreement among models will not be treated as a human reference standard or a representative estimate of performance.

Content uncertainty in records passing the abstract-availability check will be forwarded to full text. Source-based reasons, preservation of original and revised assessments, and targeted investigation of disagreements improve traceability but do not eliminate screening error. Taoka and Saito's supervisory review of final results and manuscript remains distinct from independent assessment of individual records. Prior discussion and exposure will be described accurately.

### Screening reconciliation procedure

There is no planned third adjudicator or inter-reviewer reconciliation procedure. Kado will resolve uncertain cases by rechecking original material and the criteria, documenting alternative readings and the reason for his final decision. AI-human disagreement will be resolved through this source-based human assessment, not majority voting among AI outputs. Supervisory discussion will be identified as such. A systematic ambiguity requiring a material rule change will pause the affected work for a dated amendment and reassessment of earlier affected decisions.

### Sampling and sample size

Every retrieved, deduplicated screening record passing the abstract-availability check will enter the first-pass assessment; there is no sampling among these records for AI screening and no early stopping. Availability exclusions are counted separately and remain outside the AI validation and audit frames. Sampling is used only for the prespecified human reference validation and audit of proposed exclusions. Their actual frames, denominators, seeds, selected identifiers, human judgments, AI versions and all revisions will be retained. Targeted boundary checks will remain distinct from random samples.

All studies found eligible through the completed selection procedure will be considered for synthesis, without thematic or convenience sampling of eligible studies. The validation and audit sample sizes do not establish a numerical recovery threshold, a confidence guarantee or representation of every qualitative perspective. Unresolved source comparisons will remain unresolved rather than being filled by inference.

### Screening procedure justification

The procedure uses AI to assess the entire title-and-abstract set remaining after the recorded availability check, and uses human work for reference validation, exclusion auditing, problematic cases and every final full-text decision. It aims to reduce repeated human assessment of clearly irrelevant metadata while retaining content uncertainty for full-text checking. The separate availability restriction reduces the set that can enter this workflow and may lose relevant evidence; it is not a finding of substantive irrelevance. This is an AI first-pass workflow with sampled human checks, not supervised active-learning prioritization or independent duplicate human screening.

The complete workflow, including input preparation, reference judgments, audits, error resolution, retrieval and full-text decisions, must be assessed for feasibility before its completion is claimed. Successful import, valid output formatting, literal quote matching or model agreement does not establish review-specific screening performance. If the prespecified checks cannot support use of the AI exclusions, those exclusions remain provisional; the review will resolve the affected set or report incomplete work. A further material method change will be recorded before the affected formal work.

### Data management and sharing

Screening records will retain source-record, report and study identifiers, discovery routes, exact input versions and hashes, abstract availability, AI service and exposed model identifiers, instructions and configuration versions, output versions, date, disposition, criterion-level reasons, supporting source spans, processing failures and corrections. Human records will additionally retain reviewer, actual assessment date, judgment, reason, source location and prior exposure. Abstract-availability exclusions, proposed title-and-abstract report-limit exclusions, proposed C1 exclusions for absent empirical or qualitative evidence, other proposed AI content exclusions, acceptance after validation, individual human exclusions, pending cases, report duplicates and full-text retrieval failures will remain distinct statuses. Report-type and empirical-status records will retain the non-journal or non-empirical reason, supporting source and venue evidence, conflicts, reviewer status and any later correction; an AI proposal is not an individual human decision. Availability dispositions will retain the applicable route and attempt evidence, identity or text issues, actual date and later reopening if an abstract is recovered.

Validation and audit records will preserve sampling frames, seeds, selected identifiers, human assessments made before AI inspection, comparison tables, detected errors, affected-set definitions, remedial work and the basis for accepting or declining the resulting exclusion set. Counts will distinguish discovery source rows, deduplicated reports, abstract-availability exclusions and unresolved availability cases, records processed by AI, unique records actually assessed by Kado, repeated human checks, sampled checks, AI-only content exclusions accepted after validation, reports sought, full-text retrieval failures and final human full-text decisions. Overlap with earlier preparatory content proposals will remain traceable without counting a report twice in selection accounting. A repeated check is not another unique screened report, and an AI output is not a human decision.

The review will report the availability of its data and materials and provide supplementary information where required by the applicable reporting guidance or journal. This protocol does not promise publication of all working records, source text, software or instructions. Credentials, private local paths, private reference identifiers, copyrighted abstracts and full text, private correspondence and internal working materials will not be included in public files.

### Miscellaneous screening details

Prior-knowledge records will retain their discovery route and receive assessment under the same substantive criteria. An earlier preparatory or trial label will not become a formal judgment merely by copying it into the current record.

### Entities to extract

The extraction fields and recording instructions are specified in this section and under [Extractor instructions](#extractor-instructions). We will record report and study characteristics, source evidence establishing the team's development work and shared responsibility, the practitioner's role and authority, the connection of support to that work, the empirical materials and analytic method, and the source perspective. We will distinguish support directed at development activities from support directed at the team's meetings, collaboration, learning, or ways of working, allowing both where reported.

For each relevant account we will record what is reported as noticed, who noticed it, the state, change or event and its context, and how its connection to support is established. We will distinguish the background setting from contextual conditions the practitioner is reported to consider in interpreting the situation or selecting support. For the latter, the record will retain the condition, its reported significance and the source passage linking it to that judgment; setting information alone will not establish this link. A practitioner's reported interpretation, the study authors' empirical analysis or interpretation, and the review authors' later interpretation will remain separately attributed. Where reported, we will also extract what support is provided or chosen, reasons, constraints, timing, target and extent of involvement, responses, reconsideration and the source-reported connections among these elements. Unreported optional links will remain unreported and will not exclude the account from the main organization of noticing.

Each entry will retain a report and study link, page or section/table/figure locator, a faithful paraphrase, and any qualification. An observed sequence, retrospective episode, account of a recurring state or event, conditional practice account, cross-case contrast, reconstructed vignette, and author model will remain distinguishable. An author's model or advice will be background interpretation unless its empirical basis can be identified; it will not independently satisfy eligibility or create an unreported perception or event.

Kado will read the methods and context needed to identify the eligible study component, then examine its qualitative findings and relevant tables, figures, vignettes and supplements. Empirically grounded material relevant to the question may be coded wherever it appears, including a discussion section; background literature, borrowed models and recommendations will remain distinguishable. The coding unit will be the shortest passage that preserves the relevant meaning. Kado will retain locators for adjacent context needed to identify the practitioner, referents, conditions, qualifications and source perspective, and record the material examined in a report-level coding-scope note. A short extracted summary will be a navigation aid, not the sole basis for coding without the source context. Evidence form, experience status and attribution to a practitioner or study author will be recorded separately.

The record will distinguish co-occurrence within one study from a source-reported connection among noticed content, contextual conditions, meanings and support practices for the same practitioner or situation. Each connection used in comparison will retain its source locator and attribution; unreported connections will not be filled by proximity or a shared study label. A connection created by cross-account comparison will be identified as review interpretation. When noticing and meaning overlap, their fields will reference the same source unit rather than inventing a separable cue. The worked example below illustrates source-located recording for the practitioner/group, support context, perceived content, reported meaning, connecting passage, attribution and limits. These preparatory examples illustrate recording and are not formal extractions.

For example, [Xie et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1080/03043797.2021.1946680) studied mentors supporting student teams in a 48-hour engineering design competition (pp. 953–954). On p. 959, under “Monitoring team dynamics,” the authors report mentors connecting repeated questions from team members with communication problems within a team. A worked record would retain the mentor group, competition context, repeated questioning as noticed content, communication difficulty as reported meaning, and their connection within the same source sentence. It would identify this as an author-summarized general practice account, without naming a particular mentor/team or asserting an observed episode, a cause or an intervention outcome. This preparatory illustration demonstrates recording; it is not a formal inclusion or extraction decision.

### Extraction stages

Before formal extraction, Kado will trial the form and AI instructions on a small varied set of eligible-looking reports and revise operational instructions. AI will prepare candidate extraction records from the original material, retaining source passages, locations, attribution and uncertainty. Kado will check every entry used in synthesis against the original material, including its context and qualifications, resolve report-to-study linkage and finalize the extraction. Trial material will be reassessed under the finalized instructions; changes and corrections will be logged. Material changes require a dated amendment before the affected work proceeds.

Preparatory summaries, AI suggestions and proposed codes will remain distinct from the formal source-verified analytical records. An AI output will not establish that Kado has read, checked or adopted its contents.

### Extractor instructions

The following instructions define the units, source-location requirements, missing-value rules and evidence distinctions. Reports and independent studies will be kept separate. Multiple reports with overlapping participants or observations will be linked after checking methods, setting, field dates, and reported samples. Suspected overlap will remain uncertain until resolved; title similarity alone will not establish one study. A report describing several distinct studies may link to more than one study.

A source finding is an empirically grounded claim or account concerning one practitioner or an identified practitioner group, its reported noticing and support context, and any reported interpretation, contextual grounds or support practice connected to it. Distinct practitioners, situations or perceptions will be separated where the source permits; splitting must not create a correspondence absent from the source. A synthesis finding is a review-level statement developed by comparing source findings. Neither finding counts nor internal links represent independent study counts, prevalence or effects.

Blank fields mean not yet extracted or checked. `NR` means not reported after examining the relevant available source material; `NA` means structurally inapplicable, with a reason where needed; `UNCLEAR` means examined but ambiguous, conflicting or inseparable. These values will not be used interchangeably. Each substantive entry will record the source version, page/section/table/figure or supplement locator, the responsible human and date, and its draft, unresolved or confirmed status. Study-author interpretation, practitioner meaning and review interpretation will occupy distinct fields. The lead's source recheck will not be labelled independent confirmation.

### Extractor masking

Kado will know the review questions, source identities and preparatory materials. No masking or independent duplicate extraction is planned. Supervisory review of the final results will not be reported as a second independent extraction.

### Extraction reliability

AI will prepare structured extraction candidates and identify potentially missing or contradictory source passages. Kado will verify every extraction used in synthesis against the original material, including its context, qualifications and source attribution. He will examine companion reports and possible study overlap and retain unresolved links or competing readings instead of filling gaps by inference.

There will be no independent duplicate extraction or duplicate methodological appraisal. Taoka and Saito's final supervisory review may prompt source rechecks and revisions, but will not be counted as independent confirmation of every row or judgment. AI preparation and Kado's source verification are different contributions. The risk of extraction or interpretive error will be acknowledged, with source chains, alternative readings and correction histories retained.

### Extraction reconciliation procedure

Kado will resolve extraction uncertainties by rechecking original passages and field definitions. Where the source does not decide between readings, he will retain the ambiguity or alternative interpretation. A changed definition will trigger reassessment of affected earlier entries. His final reasoning, date and changes, and any supervisory discussion, will be recorded. Missing relationships will not be completed by inference.

### Extraction procedure justification

The form preserves reported noticing and its support connection, any stated meaning, the distinction between background context and contextual grounds for judgment, optional links to support practices or responses, attribution and evidence form. This permits noticing-only accounts to contribute without treating missing links as absent practice. Rechecking all material used in synthesis and retaining a source trail support a transparent single-reviewer analysis. They do not constitute independent verification, and that limitation will be reported.

### Data management and sharing

Source documents will be stored privately, with their versions and links to review records retained. Structured review records will use UTF-8 TSV with documented identifiers and controlled values. Missing, inapplicable, unclear, and unresolved work will be distinct. Shareable data will include permissible metadata, the review lead's original paraphrases where appropriate, source locators, and study links. Copyrighted full text and private records will be excluded. The sharing destination remains to be confirmed.

### Miscellaneous extraction details

The observed or reported unit must support any proposed temporal connection. Fragments from different cases, participants, teams, or accounts will not be assembled into one team's sequence. A recollection of an earlier perception will be distinguished from evidence that an interpretation was formed only after the event; retrospective interviewing alone will not be treated as proof of post hoc justification. Reconsidered maintenance requires an explicit indication that continuation was considered and chosen; a report of unchanged practice alone is insufficient. The practitioner's perception, another team member's account, direct observation, the study authors' analysis, and the review authors' interpretation will remain separate. A report can establish a perception without establishing its accuracy or a shared team property.

### Planned data transformations

Codes, descriptive groups and higher-order themes will retain links to reports, studies and source passages. Definitions and source mappings will be versioned with reasons for changes. Mappings may be many-to-many; the two-level display does not require a single parent theme for each passage. Kado will check source-language meaning before analysis when translations are used. No quantitative effect transformation, pooled estimate or overall study-quality score is planned.

### Missing data

Unreported information will not be imputed as absent noticing, absent practice, a negative outcome, or deliberate maintenance. Interpretation, choice rationale, and response data may be missing from otherwise eligible accounts; this will be recorded and will limit only the comparisons requiring those links. Missing optional links will not be an automatic methodological-quality penalty. Unclear information and inapplicability will be recorded separately. Other reports from the same study may supply missing context if their linkage is supported. Remaining gaps will constrain the relevant claim and contribute to appraisal or confidence judgments where appropriate. There is currently no routine author-contact plan.

### Data validation

Scripts will check identifiers, cross-references, missing-value codes, required locators, duplicate records, and reported counts. Kado will verify entries against their sources and check overlapping reports for study-level double counting. Corrections and retraction notices will be checked when a report enters the included set and again before submission.

### Quality assessment

Kado will use the [JBI Checklist for Qualitative Research (2020)](https://jbi.global/sites/default/files/2020-08/Checklist_for_Qualitative_Research.pdf) for qualitative studies and the relevant qualitative component of mixed-methods studies. Before formal appraisal, he will trial the item instructions and recording form on a small varied set of reports. Item-level judgments will retain reasons and source locations. Inapplicable items will receive a reason and remain distinct from unreported information. Material changes to the appraisal plan will require prospective amendment and confirmation; trial judgments will be reassessed under the finalized instructions.

AI may locate relevant methods passages and prepare item-level evidence candidates. Kado will check those passages and make every appraisal judgment; no independent duplicate assessment is planned. No numerical total, cutoff-based exclusion or automatic weighting will be used. Methodological limitations will be distinguished from relevance to the question and richness of the reported material. Appraisal will inform study interpretation, sensitivity checks and confidence in each synthesis finding.

For each empirical synthesis finding, Kado will identify the contributing studies and material, which JBI concerns bear on that particular claim, and how the finding's content, scope and confidence change if dependence on the material is removed. Studies will not be selected by their number of Yes ratings. The contribution-and-concern record will inform CERQual methodological limitations alongside, rather than as a substitute for, coherence, adequacy and relevance.

### Synthesis plan

AI may propose source-linked codes, descriptive groups, comparison tables and alternative interpretations. Kado will examine the relevant original material and retain, revise or reject those suggestions, documenting the basis for the adopted codes and themes. He will perform thematic synthesis through source-based coding, descriptive grouping and development of higher-order themes.

1. The lead will read the relevant empirical material and its context, using the meaning units and source-scope rules specified under Entities to extract. Initial codes will retain what was noticed, the practitioner attribution and any stated significance for support, contextual grounds and support practice. Source-reported connections among these elements will remain identifiable.
2. Within each study, the lead will compare accounts and develop descriptive groups while preserving participants, teams, support contexts and evidence forms. Background context and contextual conditions used in judgment will remain distinct. Unreported meanings, practices and connections will remain identifiable rather than be supplied by the code.
3. Across studies, the lead will compare reported connections among perceived states or events, contextual conditions, interpretations and support practices. Comparisons may concern similar noticed conditions associated with similar or different meanings and practices in different contexts, or different conditions connected to a related support issue. The basis for treating noticed content or practices as comparable will be stated. The record will distinguish these comparisons from a juxtaposition of different states; a shared label or vocabulary will not establish equivalent conditions, a difference in interpretation or a connection to support. Information routes and contact conditions will be examined where reported. Workplace and educational contexts, development work, roles, authority and support targets will remain distinguishable.
4. Higher-order themes will organize the comparisons that answer the review questions. Each will have a definition, source-linked examples, the basis for grouping or separating accounts, contextual qualifications and conflicting evidence. Dependence on a single study will be stated; no minimum study count is imposed.

The planned display has two levels: descriptive code groups close to the accounts and higher-order themes. Noun phrases will label themes where they clearly preserve their meaning. These display levels do not replace the analytic steps or imply a separate first-order/second-order method. Overlapping membership, necessary detail and ungrouped material may be retained. Themes will be revised as new evidence and contrary cases require.

The report will present three connected outputs:

- An organization of reported noticing, with theme definitions, content, source-linked examples and boundaries. The two-level display will support this account, rather than serve as a contribution by itself.
- A comparison of reported connections among noticing, contextual conditions, interpretation and support practices, showing what supports each similarity or difference and preserving the development activities, support roles, settings and evidence forms involved. Accounts with partial reporting will remain visible, with the available connections and missing information identified.
- An account of the evidence and limitations for each synthesis finding, including supporting and contrary material where available, methodological concerns, gaps in the reports, contextual scope and the planned confidence assessment. An absence of reporting will not establish an absence of practice.

All eligible noticing accounts will contribute to the main organization, including accounts without an explicit meaning or later choice. Comparisons requiring contextual grounds, interpretations, specific support practices, reasons, responses or reconsideration will use the relevant available-evidence subsets. Missing links will limit only the comparisons that require them; they will not be treated as evidence of absent practice. Preparatory labels and models will not be fixed codes or eligibility criteria. Practitioners' perceptions, study-author interpretations and review-level themes will remain distinct. Separate cases or accounts will not be combined into a single sequence.

Shared codes will not alone establish a finding spanning settings. Each finding will state the practitioner perspective, development context, support relationship and authority to which it applies. Where teacher assessment authority, managerial authority or external-coach access makes accounts insufficiently comparable, context-specific explanations may remain side by side under the same organizing theme. CERQual relevance will be judged against that stated scope.

### Criteria for conclusions / inference criteria

Each synthesis finding will specify its empirical content and scope, the comparison supporting it, supporting and challenging studies, and source locators. A finding about reported noticing will not establish accurate diagnosis, a shared team state, a cause of a support choice or an improvement in development work without corresponding evidence. Descriptive counts will identify their record, report or study unit; coding and link counts will not estimate prevalence or effects.

Conclusions will answer the review question through source-supported content and comparisons, stating where evidence is insufficient. A classification label alone will not establish an empirical finding.

The planned confidence method is GRADE-CERQual for clearly specified empirical qualitative synthesis findings, considering methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy and relevance. AI may organize the contributing evidence and identify candidate concerns; Kado will verify that material and make each component and overall confidence judgment with reasons. Kado will document reasons and supporting studies in a summary of qualitative findings. A label or organizing table alone will not receive an automatic rating; the associated empirical claim must be defined. Any overarching theoretical model will remain distinct. Before formal confidence assessment, Kado will trial the CERQual guidance and recording form on clearly specified draft empirical findings. He will record judgments on methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy and relevance, and an overall confidence rating of high, moderate, low or very low with reasons. The trial remains unperformed; any material method change will require a prospective amendment and confirmation before use.

### Synthesist blinding

Kado will know the questions, sources and preparatory materials. No masking is planned. Reflexive memos will record how prior research, preparatory reading and proposed labels may shape attention and interpretation, and how contrary evidence changes the analysis.

### Synthesis reliability

Kado will adopt the formal codes, themes and synthesis findings after checking the source material, and will make the CERQual judgments. AI may prepare evidence tables and suggest alternative readings or contrary material; those outputs remain candidates until checked. He will retain the source chain, compare alternative readings, search for contrary material and examine dependence on individual studies. Taoka and Saito will review the final results and manuscript in their supervisory roles. Their comments and resulting revisions will be recorded, but this will not be reported as independent coding, duplicate synthesis or independent confidence assessment. Single-reviewer interpretation remains a limitation.

[GRADE-CERQual guidance](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0689-2) recommends discussion of each assessment by at least two review-team members. The current arrangement does not meet that recommendation: no finding-level CERQual discussion or boundary-case review has been assigned to another person. If such a contribution is later agreed, the person, scope, materials, discussion and resulting changes will be recorded prospectively. Final manuscript review alone will not be counted as that discussion.

### Synthesis reconciliation procedure

Kado will compare alternative interpretations against the source accounts and review question. He will record whether a difference is resolved by a changed code or narrower claim, retained as a competing interpretation, or left unresolved. Supervisory comments will prompt documented source rechecks where relevant; they will not create an independent adjudication process. Unresolved differences affecting a main claim will be reported and considered in its confidence judgment.

### Publication bias analyses

Funnel-plot asymmetry and small-study-effect tests are not planned for this qualitative synthesis. The review will examine the roles, contexts, sources and perspectives represented, and consider inaccessible reports, English-only full-text eligibility, missing or incorrect language metadata, journal-only and empirical-study limits, public-index coverage, self-report and selective accounts of successful or memorable practice. Excluding conference and other non-journal reports may omit relevant evidence; the restriction will be reported as a scope limitation.

The specified discovery frame, exclusion after bounded abstract retrieval, metadata availability, AI screening errors and single-reviewer judgments are additional selection limitations. Abstract-availability exclusions may differ systematically from reports with accessible abstracts; the retained record will describe their routes and available characteristics without claiming to know their unassessed findings. Human validation and exclusion audits may detect errors but do not establish the content or direction of all missed findings or guarantee a numerical recall. Reports identified outside the keyword searches will retain their discovery route and undergo the same specified eligibility process.

### Sensitivity analyses / robustness checks

Kado will re-examine each main finding using studies with fewer relevant methodological concerns, excluding practitioner self-study, separating specific episodes from recurring-state or general conditional accounts, separating workplace and educational contexts, and treating uncertain study overlap conservatively. He will compare what can be concluded from noticing-only accounts with accounts also reporting meanings, choices, or responses, without treating reporting completeness as a quality score. He will inspect dependence on any single study or closely related report family. The report will distinguish findings supported by primary, supplementary, prior-knowledge and citation discovery. It will examine whether source coverage or AI recommendations affected the evidence supporting a finding, using the retained human validation, exclusion-audit and correction records. Source-route differences do not estimate the content of undetected evidence. The required human checks are procedural requirements rather than optional sensitivity analyses. Where a planned check has too little evidence, that limitation will be stated.

### Synthesis procedure justification

Thematic synthesis fits a question about reported perceptions, interpretation in context and connections to support practices while allowing unanticipated similarities and differences. Preserving source attribution and study context permits comparison without treating practitioner accounts, observation and review interpretation as interchangeable. The two-level display will make the path from descriptive material to higher-order organization inspectable. Appraisal, confidence judgments and sensitivity checks will address the limits of the resulting empirical claims.

[Thomas and Harden (2008), Conclusion](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2288-8-45) explain that synthesis need not go beyond the content of primary studies when those studies directly address the review question. This supports a systematic descriptive answer with bounded comparisons; further conceptual development will depend on the evidence.

### Synthesis data management and sharing

The review will retain links from source records to reports, studies, extracted accounts, codes, synthesis findings, and confidence judgments. Tables and counts will be generated from the structured records. Shareable data will preserve this evidence trail while excluding copyrighted source text and private information. The final report will state where the materials are available and any access conditions.

### Miscellaneous synthesis details

No additional synthesis procedures are specified beyond those described above.
